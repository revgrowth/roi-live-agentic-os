# French Broad Chocolates — Brand Voice Guide
---
## 1. Brand Overview
French Broad Chocolates is a bean-to-bar chocolate company founded by Jael and Dan Skeffington. What began as a chocolate passion and a café in Costa Rica has grown into a 85+ person operation with an experiential chocolate factory and café in Asheville, North Carolina, a chocolate lounge and boutique in Downtown Asheville, e-commerce, wholesale, and a tours program. Named after the French Broad River that flows through Asheville, the brand embodies constant change, deep local roots, and a commitment to doing all of it — sourcing, making, transforming, and sharing — deeply.
**Always use the full name "French Broad Chocolates" in all external communications. "FBC" is internal shorthand only — never use it on the website, in marketing copy, or in customer-facing materials.**

## 2. Origin Story
### The Spark
Jael Skeffington grew up in Minnesota in a family of six where money was tight. Her mother's creative side projects meant Jael grew up "under a craft fair table," instilling an entrepreneurial spirit from childhood. While pursuing her MBA at the University of Minnesota, Jael began experimenting with chocolate as an artistic outlet. She discovered the emerging craft chocolate movement — companies like Scharfenberger and books like Alice Medrich's *Bittersweet* — and fell in love with the idea that chocolate could express the inherent flavor of cacao rather than mask it.
While making truffles by hand, Jael experienced a moment of clarity: *"Chocolate is the thing that will make me happy."* She decided to devote her life to chocolate. Dan, whom she'd met at his brother's wedding over a shared passion for food and sustainability, took her seriously — and joined her.
### Costa Rica: Bread and Chocolate
Both dropped out of their programs. They bought a retired school bus, converted it to run on used vegetable oil, and drove it from Minnesota to Puerto Viejo, Costa Rica — discovering five days before departure that Jael was pregnant with their first child. In Puerto Viejo, they rented a small café called Café Corral and transformed it into *Bread and Chocolate*, a restaurant built on homemade bread, whole foods, and handmade desserts. It was here they first tasted fresh cacao from local farms, visited open-fire roasting operations, and fell in love with chocolate at its source. Customers kept telling them: *"This reminds me of a café in my hometown."* The founders realized that food could be connection and belonging.
### Asheville: Finding Home
After two years, customers kept recommending Asheville. They visited for five days in 2006 and knew it was home — a creative community that supported small businesses and valued local food. They sold Bread and Chocolate, drove the bus back to the US, and settled in Asheville. Their first year was a "research year" — a home-based, certified organic kitchen selling at farmers' markets. When the model proved unsustainable, a friend told them: *"You can't quit. The world needs your chocolate."*
With help from Mountain Bizworks, they wrote a business plan for the French Broad Chocolate Lounge, secured a loan from Asheville Savings Bank just before the 2008 recession, and built out a small underground space themselves. Lines formed immediately. The lounge expanded upstairs, then added a pastry kitchen.
### Bean to Bar
By 2009–2010, they realized they weren't connected to the source of their chocolate. Inspired by pioneers like Dandelion, Dick Taylor, Fruition, and Ritual, they spent two years experimenting — selling hand-wrapped bars made in their home kitchen — before completing their first chocolate factory in 2012. They established direct-sourcing relationships with cacao producers who shared their values, and the identity shifted: from a retail shop that sold chocolate to a *chocolate manufacturer with a retail shop*.
### Resilience
Hurricane Helene in 2024 flooded their factory and devastated their retail revenue model. But the production facility — sitting three feet higher — survived. The community's response was "instant and inspiring." The crisis taught them the value of diversification, interdependence, and showing up for each other. They rebuilt, diversified into online and wholesale channels, and emerged more resilient.

## 3. Mission & Purpose
French Broad Chocolates exists to create meaningful experiences with chocolate — experiences rooted in pleasure, connection, and integrity. They source cacao with care, make chocolate from scratch, transform it into bars, bonbons, brownies, pastries, and drinking chocolates, and invite people into their spaces to experience the whole story.
**Core belief:** Chocolate is a food, not just a sweet. It is shaped by people, place, and process. Knowing where something comes from changes how you experience it.
---
## 4. Brand Values
### Connection to Source
Directly sourcing cacao for flavor, origin, and the specific qualities of a particular farm. Building relationships with farmers and producers. Understanding that cacao is an agricultural product shaped by genetics, terroir, and the expertise of the people who grow, ferment, and dry it.
### Craftsmanship
Treating chocolate making as the intersection of art and science. Processes built on human discernment, tradition, and expertise — preserving what's already in the cacao bean rather than engineering homogeneity.
### Pleasure & Accessibility
Great sourcing and great craft should lead to something deeply delicious and accessible — not something that needs explaining before you're allowed to like it. Chocolate should bring joy, not require a lecture.
### Sustainability & Fair Partnership
Paying above commodity prices — not as charity, but because quality cacao comes from skilled farmers and fermenters who deserve to be paid for their expertise. Seeking out farms with healthy agroforestry practices, shade-grown cacao, and biodiverse systems that store carbon and support the land.
### Community
Asheville gave French Broad Chocolates everything. Being an active part of its growth and resilience is core to who they are. The business belongs to the community as much as it belongs to the founders. Collaboration is part of the DNA.
### Transparency
Telling the story of where chocolate comes from — the origin, the farmers, the process. Comparing the experience to visiting a brewery taproom, a farmer's market, or opening a CSA box. The origin matters to the flavor, to the story, and to the people who worked so hard to produce it.
### Evolution
The French Broad River is a metaphor for life and business — it represents the only real constant, which is change. The brand embraces continuous improvement and lifelong learning.

## 6. Voice & Tone
### Voice (Consistent)
French Broad Chocolates sounds like a knowledgeable friend who happens to make incredible chocolate — someone who's passionate without being pretentious, informed without being academic, and warm without being saccharine. The voice is:
- **Conversational and human** — not corporate, not clinical
- **Confident but not boastful** — they know what they do well and why it matters
- **Story-driven** — always grounding claims in real experiences, real people, real places
- **Sensory and evocative** — chocolate is a physical, emotional experience; the language should reflect that
- **Inclusive** — welcoming everyone to the table, not just connoisseurs
### Tone (Shifts by Context)
| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| **Product descriptions** | Sensory, inviting, warm. Lead with flavor and feeling, then layer in origin and craft. |
| **Origin/sourcing stories** | Reverent, specific, human. Name the farmers, the country, the process. Show respect without romanticizing. |
| **Social media** | Playful, personal, behind-the-scenes. Let the team's personality shine. |
| **Educational content (tours, tastings)** | Curious, generous, approachable. Teach by sharing wonder, not by lecturing. |
| **Crisis/community moments** | Candid, grateful, resilient. Acknowledge difficulty honestly. Express gratitude without performing it. |
| **Wholesale/B2B** | Professional but still warm. Lead with quality, craft, and shared values. |

## 7. Messaging Framework
### Elevator Pitch
French Broad Chocolates is a bean-to-bar chocolate company in Asheville, North Carolina. We source the finest cacao from farmers in Costa Rica, Guatemala, Nicaragua, and Peru, make many varieties of chocolate from scratch, and transform it into bars, bonbons, brownies, pastries, and drinking chocolates. We invite people into our spaces to experience the whole story — from bean to bar to bite.
### Key Messages
1. **We do all of it, and we do it deeply.** Source cacao with integrity. Make chocolate from scratch with care. Turn it into something beautiful. Invite people in to experience the whole story.
2. **Chocolate is a food, not just a sweet.** It's an agricultural product shaped by people, place, and process — just like wine, coffee, or bread.
3. **Knowing where something comes from changes how you experience it.** We tell the story of the farmers, the land, and the craft behind every piece.
4. **Great sourcing and great craft should lead to something deeply delicious.** Not something that needs explaining before you're allowed to like it.
5. **We're not trying to win on price.** You're paying for the sourcing, the craft, the people, the spaces, and the way we show up in our community.
6. **Collaboration is part of our DNA.** We are better together — with our farmers, our team, our community, and our industry.
7. **Slow down. Taste with intention.** Experience chocolate like you would a fine wine — with attention, curiosity, and pleasure.

## 8. Language Guidelines
### Words & Phrases We Use
- "Connection to source"
- "Relationships with farmers and producers"
- "Bean to bar"
- "Craft chocolate"
- "Terroir" / "Sense of place"
- "People, place, and process"
- "Deeply delicious"
- "Taste with intention"
- "The whole story"
- "Intersection of art and science"
- "Edible jewels" / "Small sculptures" (for bonbons)
- "Experiential chocolate factory"
- "Joy, connection, offering"
- "Heart-centered"
- "Making food with intention"
- "Continuous improvement"
### Words & Phrases We Avoid
- **"FBC"** — internal only, never in external communications
- **"Luxury" or "indulgence"** as primary descriptors — chocolate is a food, not a status symbol
- **"Artisanal" without substance** — don't use it as a vague marketing word; ground it in specifics
- **"Farm to table"** — overused; prefer "connection to source" or "bean to bar"
- **"Guilt-free"** — there's nothing to feel guilty about
- **"Sinful" / "decadent"** — these cheapen the experience and contradict the food-first philosophy
- **"Premium" as a standalone claim** — show, don't tell; describe what makes it exceptional
- **Corporate jargon** — no "leveraging synergies" or "disrupting the space"
- **Condescending or gatekeeping language** — never make someone feel they need credentials to enjoy chocolate
- **Overly technical cacao terminology without context** — if you use a term like "conching" or "Criollo," explain it warmly
### Formatting Preferences
- Use the full name "French Broad Chocolates" on first reference; "French Broad" is acceptable on subsequent references
- Capitalize "Chocolate Lounge" and "Chocolate Factory" when referring to their specific locations
- Cacao-producing countries and farm names should always be named specifically — never generalize to "our farms" without attribution

## 9. Competitive Positioning
### What We Are
- A craft chocolate maker that sources, makes, transforms, and shares — all under one roof
- Pioneers of the experiential chocolate factory model — a space designed to be walked through, experienced, and shared
- A company that balances purity with pleasure, craft with accessibility
- Deeply embedded in the Asheville food ecosystem and community
- Award-winning (Good Food Awards) with nearly 20 years of history
### What We Are Not
- An industrial chocolate company engineering for homogeneity and cost efficiency
- A "purity-first" craft brand that sacrifices pleasure for ideology
- A narrow connoisseur brand that appeals only to experts
- A company competing on price
### How We're Different
1. **Vertical integration:** Most chocolate companies do one thing. We do all of it — sourcing, manufacturing, confections, pastry, retail, tours, education — and we do it deeply.
2. **Experiential spaces:** Our factory is big and beautiful by design. It's meant to be walked through, experienced, and shared — not hidden behind closed doors.
3. **Pleasure-forward craft:** We believe some in the craft chocolate world have become very focused on purity, sometimes at the expense of pleasure. We make things that make people happy — not just things that appeal to a narrow connoisseur audience.
4. **Storytelling in sourcing:** We don't just name an origin. We tell the story of the farmers, the fermenters, the land, and the decisions that shaped the flavor.
5. **Community as core business:** Our investment in Asheville, in collaboration, and in showing up for our city isn't a CSR initiative — it's who we are.

## 12. The River Metaphor
The French Broad River is more than a namesake — it's the brand's central metaphor. One of the oldest rivers in the world, it flows through Asheville and represents:
- **Constant change** — the business has evolved from a school bus to a café to a home kitchen to a lounge to a factory to a multi-channel operation
- **Deep roots** — the river has been here for hundreds of millions of years, just as the brand is built for longevity
- **Connection** — the river connects communities, just as chocolate connects people to farmers, to place, and to each other
- **Resilience** — the river flooded their factory and they rebuilt; the river keeps flowing and so do they
Use the river as a touchstone in brand storytelling when discussing evolution, resilience, or connection to Asheville.
