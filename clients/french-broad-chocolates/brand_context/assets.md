## Sourcing Partners Referenced (from Dan's transcript)

Cacao supply chain — for transparency content and storytelling:

- **Cacao Bisiesto** (Matagalpa, Nicaragua) — partnership between a Nicaraguan agronomist and an American expat. Direct importing relationship since ~2012, ~14 years. Most direct + longest-running supplier.
- **Cooperativa Norandino** (formerly CEPICAFE; Northern Peru) — large cooperative-of-cooperatives. First Peru source, ~2011–2012.
- **Cooperativa Pangoa** (Satipo region, Central Peru) — collected via cacao explorer Steve Bergen. Multi-year imports. Currently dormant relationship.
- **Daniel South** (Puerto Viejo, Talamanca, Costa Rica) — supplier from 2004 to 2023. Passed away in 2024. Quote-worthy: "He was 63 when he died… he will be missed. It's a big loss for that region." A new relationship is in development with growers in the indigenous Bribri region near Puerto Viejo.
- **Nutribody / Diego** (Lima, Peru) — processes Pangoa + Atalaya cacao into cocoa butter and cocoa powder.
- **Cacao Zorzal / Dr. Charles Kirchner** (Dominican Republic) — operates a private rainforest reserve protecting migratory thrush. Songbird namesake.
- **Rizek** (Dominican Republic) — one of the largest private family-owned cacao producers; conduit for cocoa butter + powder to the US East Coast.

**Live website lists current origin countries as Nicaragua, Dominican Republic, and Uganda.** The Brand Voice Guide v1 says Costa Rica, Guatemala, Nicaragua, and Peru. **Discrepancy flag** — origin lineup has clearly shifted post-2024 (Daniel South, Helene, cacao price spike). Uganda is new and not yet covered in any input transcript. *Tracked in open-gaps as CRIT 01.*

### Local + regional partners (from confex transcript)

Named ingredient suppliers from the confections team's own words. These become content gold for Cluster 09 (Bonbons) supporting articles and product-page sourcing notes.

- **Step Family Farms** — local Asheville-area raspberries. *"We buy when they're in season and freeze puree and freeze so that we can use our local berries all year round and not just when they're in season."* (Edgar) Used in the Raspberry bonbon, one of FBC's earliest signature pieces.
- **Freddy's Guys** (Oregon) — hazelnuts. Edgar's longtime preferred source — *"I've used their hazelnuts for years here. And back when I lived in Oregon, those hazelnuts…"* Used in the Hazelnut Praline bonbon and in the milk-chocolate caramelized hazelnut treats.
- **Spice Walla** — spices. Named partner, values-aligned.
- **Burlap and Barrel** — spices. Named partner, values-aligned.
- **Oak & Grist Distillery** (Asheville) — whiskey. Used in the Asheville Collection caramel bonbon.
- **"Palm Creek" honey** (verify spelling — possibly Pell Creek; tracked as M07 in open-gaps) — local Asheville-area honey. Used in the Wildflower Honey Caramel — FBC's first signature bonbon, originated in Jael's home kitchen in 2007.

## Timeline (canonical dates pulled from sources)

- **~2003** — Jael in MBA program at U of Minnesota; chocolate-truffle moment. Dan in law school. Both drop out.
- **2004** — Drove school bus from Minnesota to Costa Rica; opened Bread & Chocolate (Café Corral, Puerto Viejo). First son born in apartment above the café.
- **2006** — Sold Bread & Chocolate; moved to Asheville. Year-one farmer's market stand.
- **2007** — Opened French Broad Chocolate Lounge (original "hobbit hole" underground location). $60K loan from Asheville Savings Bank, just before 2008 credit freeze. Second son born.
- **2009–2010** — Began bean-to-bar experimentation; hand-wrapped bars sold at the Lounge.
- **2012** — First chocolate factory completed.
- **~2014** — Chocolate Lounge moved to current Pack Square location.
- **2018** — Current Chocolate Factory at 821 Riverside Drive opened.
- **March 2020** — COVID disruption.
- **2024** — Daniel South (Costa Rica supplier) passes away.
- **2024** — Cacao commodity market triples — peaks at ~$12,000/ton from ~$3,000/ton.
- **2024-09-27** — Hurricane Helene flood. River climbs the front door 39 inches; 8 inches of water inside the building. Production floor untouched (3 ft higher).
- **2025** — Rebuild year. Lost ~$1M in retail; nearly recovered through online + wholesale.
- **2026** — Operating with 85+ team members. Boutique reopening underway. First year able to set 3-year plan again.

## Design Reference (for future HTML deliverables)

**Approved aesthetic — confirmed by client 2026-04-27 for use in HTML deliverables produced for French Broad Chocolates:**

- **Reference page:** https://roi.live/fbc/bonbon-subscriptions.html
- **Local screenshot:** `/mnt/c/Users/jason/Downloads/Screenshot_27-4-2026_163236_roi.live.jpeg`

Visual notes (for future builds):
- **Palette:** deep chocolate-brown ground, cream/warm off-white card surfaces, tan/khaki accents, soft sky-blue accent section as occasional contrast.
- **Type:** editorial serif for display ("A dozen hand-crafted bonbons. Every month."), clean sans for body and UI.
- **Layout:** wide editorial grid, generous whitespace between sections, numbered storytelling blocks (01 / 02 / 03), founder photo-quote block, plan-comparison cards, three-step "how it works" row, photography-forward hero and section breaks.
- **Tone of imagery:** product macro on dark backgrounds, founder candid (school bus, factory floor), bonbon top-downs, hands-in-process shots.
- **CTA pattern:** orange-tan primary buttons against deep brown.
- **Footer pattern:** "Good questions" FAQ accordion, then a closing hero ("Twelve new bonbons, every month.") with primary CTA.

Apply this aesthetic — palette, type pairing, editorial rhythm, photography-led storytelling — when producing the leadership-grade HTML brand foundation document and any future client-facing HTML deliverables. Do not deviate without checking in.
