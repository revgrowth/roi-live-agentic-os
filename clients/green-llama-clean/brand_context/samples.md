# Green Llama Clean — Voice Samples

> Three short exemplars of the GLC editorial voice with annotations on the patterns they demonstrate. Drawn from existing GL brand documents and published material that meets the editorial overlay.

## Sample 1 — Mission framing

From [GL_Blog_Writing_Guidelines_v1.md](./sops/_archive/GL_Blog_Writing_Guidelines_v1.md) §2:

> "Non-toxic is not a marketing claim at Green Llama. It is a formulation principle."

**What this demonstrates.** The signature GLC pattern: take a category buzzword, refuse the marketing reading of it, and reframe it as an operational principle the company actually executes against. Two short sentences. No softeners. No throat-clearing. The reader closes the gap on their own. This is the rhythm to mirror in any homepage hero, any positioning paragraph, any About-page line that has to do real work in 20 words or fewer.

## Sample 2 — Definitional BLUF

From the published example BLUF in [GL_Blog_Content_SOP_v1_1.md](./sops/GL_Blog_Content_SOP_v1_1.md) § "BLUF Paragraph Rules":

The published BLUF on "non-toxic laundry detergent" answers the search query directly by:

- Defining the term as a product formulated without ingredients known or suspected to cause harm through normal use
- Naming three specific exclusion categories: cancer-linked compounds (1,4-dioxane), endocrine disruptors (phthalates in synthetic fragrance), respiratory and skin sensitizers
- Doing all of that in two sentences, around 50 words, structured for featured-snippet extraction

**What this demonstrates.** The BLUF answers the search query directly, names specific chemicals (entity SEO + AEO extraction), and treats the reader as informed enough to handle real chemistry vocabulary with brief in-line context. No fear-mongering: the chemicals are named with their evidence-strength language ("known or suspected"), not catastrophized. Note that this is also a working three-item factual enumeration (cancer / endocrine / respiratory and skin) of the kind the C8 and D2 rulings explicitly permit.

## Sample 3 — Founder voice

From [about-kay-baker.md](../../../../tmp/glc-onboarding/green-llama-package/03-knowledge-base/about-kay-baker.md):

The Kay Baker voice in editorial contexts pairs:

- Concrete professional grounding (occupational therapist, Master's from Louisiana State University Health Sciences Center, pediatric career background)
- Lived-experience anchor (she and Matt converted their backyard garage into a laboratory at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic)
- Specific operational signals (rigorous formula development against EPA Safer Choice criteria, Leaping Bunny certification, eliminating unnecessary plastics)

**What this demonstrates.** First-person credentialed expertise paired with a lived experience anchor is the E-E-A-T signal the brand runs on. Kay's voice carries a specific lens — OT, parent — that no generic eco-clean brand can claim. The published bio names credentials, institution, and the specific moment that triggered the founding. Three E-E-A-T signals in one paragraph without ever using the phrase "E-E-A-T."

## Negative samples — what does NOT meet the editorial overlay

The Performance Lab pillar at [reference/samples/pillar-performance-lab-2026-Q1.md](./reference/samples/pillar-performance-lab-2026-Q1.md) is currently published but flagged for remediation. It demonstrates what the editorial overlay specifically rules out:

- Sources dumped at the bottom with no inline citation hooks (violates agency Citation Discipline SOP §7.4)
- Word count ~1,400 (under the e-comm pillar band of 2,500–3,500)
- Em-dashes throughout (violates the GLC overlay full ban — ruling C2)
- An opening rhetorical fragment ("If it doesn't smell like a chemical factory and burn your eyes, it isn't working.") used as a binary contrast (banned per Core Standards §8.2)

The remediation task is captured in [`context/open-items.md`](../context/open-items.md). Until the pillar is fixed, no new spokes that link to it should be written; they would inherit the credibility hit.
