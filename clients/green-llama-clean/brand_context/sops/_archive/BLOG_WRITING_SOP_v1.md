# Green Llama Blog Writing SOP — v1

This is the operational SOP for any blog content destined for the Green Llama Clean website. It applies to every article, every newsletter post, every long-form piece bylined under Kay Baker.

## Byline structure

Every article opens with:

```
**By Kay Baker, MS, OTR/L | Reviewed by Matthew Keasey, Ph.D.**
```

Kay is the author. Matt is the scientific reviewer. This pairing is the E-E-A-T anchor.

## BLUF opening

Every article opens with a Bottom Line Up Front paragraph:

- One paragraph
- Sized as a featured snippet candidate (40–60 words is the sweet spot)
- States the answer to the search query directly
- Sets the rest of the article up as the supporting case

The BLUF is not a teaser. It is the answer. The rest of the article exists to substantiate it.

## E-E-A-T compliance

**Experience.** Kay's professional context (occupational therapist, founder, parent) is the lens. Specific, concrete experience-grounded claims beat generic statements every time.

**Expertise.** Matt's scientific reviewer role is implicit in every cited claim. The article should sound like it has been pressure-tested by a Ph.D.

**Authoritativeness.** Tier 1 sources only. EPA, NIH, peer-reviewed studies, EWG, recognized industry bodies. Generic ".org" sources are not Tier 1.

**Trustworthiness.** Real, verifiable URLs. No "[citation pending]" passing as a finished article. If a claim cannot be sourced cleanly, it gets the `[STAT NEEDED]` flag for review.

## The [STAT NEEDED] protocol

Never fabricate a statistic. Never round a number to make it more compelling. Never cite a study you have not verified exists.

**When uncertain, flag.** The format is:

```
[STAT NEEDED: specific claim that needs sourcing]
```

This goes inline in the draft. Reviewers will replace it with a verified citation or remove the claim before publish.

This protocol is non-negotiable. Hallucinated citations are the single most damaging content problem on this account.

## Tier 1 sourcing requirements

Real, verifiable URLs. No 404s. No dead links. No paraphrased citations of studies that the author has not opened.

Acceptable Tier 1 sources:
- EPA (epa.gov) — Safer Choice product list, indoor air quality, VOC research
- NIH / PubMed (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) — peer-reviewed studies
- EWG (ewg.org) — Healthy Cleaning database, ingredient ratings
- Peer-reviewed journals — direct DOI links preferred
- Recognized industry bodies — relevant standards organizations

Unacceptable as Tier 1:
- Generic ".org" sources without subject authority
- Wikipedia
- Marketing blogs from unrelated brands
- LLM-generated summaries presented as research

When citing EPA documents, use the specific report title and year (e.g., "EPA 2014 Indoor Air Quality Report") rather than a generic landing page link. AI answer engines attribute claims more confidently to specific reports than to general pages.

## Voice and writing rules

These are layered on top of Jason's general writing rules. Specific to blog content for Green Llama:

- **Direct tone.** State facts. Trust the reader.
- **Varied sentence length.** No three consecutive sentences of the same length.
- **No adverbs.** Particularly no -ly intensifiers ("incredibly," "extremely," "actually").
- **No em-dashes.** Use sentence breaks or commas.
- **No throat-clearing openers.** Get to the point.
- **No softeners.** "Just," "simply," "really," "very," "quite."
- **No meta-commentary.** "Let me explain" / "Here's why that matters" / "It's worth noting."
- **No passive voice.**
- **No narrator-from-a-distance.** "One might argue" / "Some say."
- **No banned phrases.** See `01-project-instructions/WRITING_RULES.md` for the full list.

## Product positioning constraints

- **Fragrance-free positioning.** Green Llama formulations are positioned as fragrance-free. Marketing language should not claim or imply scent-based cleaning effectiveness.
- **No liquid product mentions for hard-surface formulations.** Surface cleaner content should reference the tablet-based system, not liquids.
- **Specific, real product mentions.** When a product is named in an article, it must match what is actually for sale on the site. Pricing, sizes, certifications, and packaging details must be current.
- **EWG Verified, EPA Safer Choice, Leaping Bunny, etc.** — only cite a certification if the specific product carries it.

## Internal linking

Every article in the topical cluster carries internal links to:
- The pillar article (always)
- 2–4 supporting articles in the same cluster
- 1–2 articles in adjacent clusters where contextually appropriate
- The relevant product or collection page where mention is organic

**Anchor text matters.** Use exact-match or close-variant target keywords as anchor text where natural. Avoid "click here" / "our guide" / "this article" generic anchors.

A typical cluster article carries 10–14 internal links. Each link must be relevant to the surrounding context — not appended at the end as a "related reading" dump (though a related reading section at the end is fine).

## Word count

Cluster articles target 2,000–2,500 words. The pillar article targets 4,000+ words. Word count is a function of search intent depth, not a target for its own sake.

## Featured snippet optimization

Each article contains at least one section structured as a featured snippet candidate:
- A clear question or definition heading
- A 40–60 word answer immediately under the heading
- Supporting detail in the paragraphs that follow

The BLUF often serves this role. Additional snippet candidates can appear at section headings.

## Frequently asked questions section

Every cluster article ends with a 3–6 question FAQ section. Questions should be drawn from real search queries — People Also Ask, Reddit threads, Quora, customer support emails. Answers should be 50–100 words.

## Related reading section

After the FAQ, a "Related Reading" section listing 4–6 internal links to other cluster content. This is the explicit linking section. The inline links throughout the article are the primary link distribution; the related reading section is reinforcement.

## Final pre-publish checklist

Before any article gets published, verify:

- [ ] Byline matches exactly: "By Kay Baker, MS, OTR/L | Reviewed by Matthew Keasey, Ph.D."
- [ ] BLUF opening is 40–60 words and answers the search query
- [ ] No `[STAT NEEDED]` flags remain
- [ ] All cited URLs return 200 status codes
- [ ] All cited studies are real and accessible
- [ ] No banned phrases or structures from `WRITING_RULES.md`
- [ ] No adverbs, em-dashes, softeners, or throat-clearing
- [ ] Product mentions match current site inventory
- [ ] No fragrance-based marketing language
- [ ] No liquid product mentions for hard-surface formulations
- [ ] 10–14 internal links present, contextually placed
- [ ] FAQ section present with 3–6 questions
- [ ] Related reading section present with 4–6 internal links
- [ ] Word count within 2,000–2,500 range (cluster) or 4,000+ (pillar)
