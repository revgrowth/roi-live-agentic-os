> **WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS**
>
> This rules document was created in direct response to feedback from Green Llama's co-founders, Matt Keasey (Chief Science Officer, Ph.D.) and Kay Baker (CEO/Co-Founder, MS, OTR/L). Their core concerns: published blog content contained factual inaccuracies about Green Llama's products, fabricated statistics, and citations to studies that do not exist. These rules are mandatory and non-negotiable for all future content.

## 1. Product Accuracy

### 1.1 Green Llama Does NOT Make Liquid Products

This is the single most important product fact to get right. Green Llama's entire mission and business model is built around eliminating liquid cleaning products. Their product line is **powder, tablets, and solid formats only**. Referencing Green Llama as having or recommending a liquid product is a fundamental misrepresentation of the brand.

> **HARD RULE**
>
> **Never describe, imply, or depict Green Llama as making, selling, or recommending any liquid product.** This includes liquid detergent, liquid soap, liquid refills, or any liquid concentrate. The only exception is their spray cleaning products (which use reusable glass bottles with tablet refills, not liquid refill pouches).

### 1.2 Know the Actual Product Line

Before writing any content that references Green Llama products, confirm the product details are accurate. As of March 2026, Green Llama's core products include:

- **Laundry Powder**: concentrated, unscented, plastic-free packaging
- **Dishwasher Tablets**: EWG Verified, plastic-free, no PVA film
- **Spray Cleaning Kit**: reusable glass bottles + dissolvable cleaning tablets (multi-surface, glass, bathroom)
- **Wool Dryer Balls**: natural, reusable alternative to dryer sheets
- **Bamboo Scrub Brushes**: plastic-free kitchen cleaning

If you are unsure whether a product exists or what format it comes in, **check greenllamaclean.com before writing**. Do not guess.

### 1.3 Product Description Rules

| # | Rule | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No liquid products | Never say Green Llama makes, sells, or recommends liquid detergent, liquid soap, or liquid refills. Their model is specifically anti-liquid. |
| 2 | Use correct product names | Reference the actual product name as listed on greenllamaclean.com. Do not invent product names or SKUs. |
| 3 | Formats must match reality | If describing Green Llama's laundry detergent, say "powder" or "concentrated powder." Never say "pods," "sheets," or "liquid." |
| 4 | Certifications must be current | Only reference certifications Green Llama actually holds (e.g., Leaping Bunny, WBENC). Do not attribute certifications they haven't earned. |
| 5 | Pricing and availability | Do not fabricate pricing, store availability, or partnership claims. If unknown, omit. |

## 2. No Fabricated Data, Statistics, or Citations

This is the second critical issue raised by the client. Content has included statistics, studies, and data points that **do not exist**. This is unacceptable. Fabricated data damages the brand's scientific credibility, especially given that Green Llama's Chief Science Officer is a Ph.D. chemist who reviews every published article.

### 2.1 Examples of Violations (From Client Feedback)

Kay Baker flagged these specific hallucinations found in published content:

> **FABRICATED CITATION #1**
> *"A lifecycle analysis published in the Journal of Industrial Ecology found that transportation accounted for 10 to 15% of the total environmental impact of liquid household cleaning products."*
>
> This study does not exist. The journal name is real, but the specific study, data point, and finding were invented by the AI.

> **FABRICATED CITATION #2**
> *"Americans throw away an estimated 2.5 billion plastic cleaning bottles every year, according to data from the EPA's municipal solid waste reports."*
>
> This specific statistic cannot be verified in EPA reports. The number was fabricated.

### 2.2 The Zero-Fabrication Standard

Every piece of data in Green Llama content must pass this test:

- Can I find this exact statistic in a real, verifiable source?
- Can I provide a working URL or DOI to the source?
- Does the source actually say what I'm claiming it says?
- Is the source a recognized, credible institution (not a blog or aggregator)?

If the answer to any of these is no, do not include the data point.

### 2.3 Approved Source Types

When citing data, only use sources from this hierarchy:

- **Tier 1 (Preferred):** Peer-reviewed journals via PubMed/NCBI, EPA.gov, EWG.org, FDA.gov
- **Tier 2 (Acceptable):** University research (.edu), WHO, established industry bodies, PubChem, ChemExpo Knowledgebase
- **Tier 3 (Use with caution):** Major news outlets reporting on studies (always trace back to the original study)
- **Never acceptable:** AI-generated summaries, unverifiable blog posts, Wikipedia (as a primary source), social media

### 2.4 Source Citation Requirements

Per Kay Baker's direct request: if we reference a study, include a source at the bottom of the article. Every article that cites data must include a "Sources" section at the end with:

- The full name of the study or report
- The publishing body or journal
- A working URL or DOI link
- The year of publication

## 3. Image Accuracy

AI-generated images for Green Llama content must not contradict the brand's product reality.

### 3.1 Image Hard Rules

- **No images of liquid detergent bottles**, jugs, or liquid pour shots
- **No images of plastic packaging** (contradicts the brand's zero-plastic mission)
- **No images depicting Green Llama products** unless the depiction is verified accurate
- **No images showing pricing**, cost comparisons, or dollar amounts
- **No text overlay in AI-generated images** (AI text is unreliable)
- **DO use:** Natural materials, botanicals, abstract/architectural visuals, powder textures, clean fabrics, nature scenes
- **DO use:** Conceptual imagery that supports the article topic without depicting specific products

## 4. Mandatory Writing Process

To prevent the issues above from recurring, every article must go through these steps:

### 4.1 Pre-Writing Checklist

- Verify every product reference against greenllamaclean.com
- Research statistics independently using approved Tier 1/2 sources (see Section 2.3)
- Confirm every cited study exists by locating the actual publication with a URL/DOI
- Cross-check brand certifications are current and accurately described
- Review AI image prompts for anything that contradicts product reality (no liquids, no plastic)

### 4.2 The "Would Matt Catch This?" Test

Matthew Keasey, Ph.D., reviews every published article. Before submitting any draft, ask yourself: *Would Green Llama's Chief Science Officer flag this as inaccurate?* If there's any doubt about a claim, a statistic, or a product description, verify it or remove it. It is always better to omit a data point than to fabricate one.

### 4.3 When In Doubt, Leave It Out

If you cannot verify a statistic, study, or product detail within 5 minutes of searching approved sources, do not include it. Write around the gap using:

- General scientific principles (e.g., "plant-based surfactants biodegrade more readily than petroleum-based alternatives")
- Certification references (e.g., "EWG Verified means every ingredient meets strict health and safety criteria")
- Qualitative statements grounded in known facts rather than invented quantitative claims
- Placeholder brackets like `[STAT NEEDED – verify before publish]` to flag gaps for the team

## 5. Quick-Reference: Do / Don't

| ✅ DO | ❌ DON'T |
|---|---|
| "Green Llama's concentrated laundry powder…" | "Green Llama's liquid detergent…" |
| Cite a real EPA page with a URL | Say "according to the EPA" without a specific, verifiable source |
| "Plant-based surfactants biodegrade more readily than petroleum-based ones" | "Studies show 73% of plant-based surfactants biodegrade within 48 hours" (if you can't source it) |
| Include a Sources section with links at the bottom of every article | Assume the reader won't check your citations |
| Use images of natural materials, powder textures, clean fabrics | Use images of liquid bottles, plastic jugs, or product mockups |
| Write `[STAT NEEDED]` when you can't find a verified number | Invent a plausible-sounding statistic |
| Confirm certifications on the Green Llama website | Attribute certifications Green Llama hasn't earned (e.g., B Corp, EPA Safer Choice on products that aren't certified) |

## 6. Accountability

These rules exist because inaccurate content costs the Green Llama team significant time to correct and undermines the trust we're building through SEO and entity authority. Every article that requires a full rewrite is time Kay and Matt could spend growing the business.

The standard is simple: every fact must be true, every product reference must be accurate, and every citation must point to a real source. Content that doesn't meet this standard will not be published.
