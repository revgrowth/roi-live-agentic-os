## 1. About Green Llama

Green Llama is a certified Women-Owned business that makes zero-waste, plant and mineral-based, non-toxic home cleaning products. It was co-founded by Kay Baker, MS, OTR/L and Matthew Keasey, Ph.D., a molecular neuroscientist. Every product in the Green Llama lineup was designed from the ground up with one principle: cleaning your home with effective cleaning products that will not expose your family, pets, or the planet to harmful chemicals.

Green Llama products are formulated with plant and mineral-based ingredients. The entire product line is Leaping Bunny Certified (cruelty-free). Currently, the Dishwasher Tabs, the new Laundry Powder, and the Dishwasher Powder are EWG Verified. Green Llama uses compostable packaging and concentrated tablets and powders designed to eliminate single-use plastic waste and reduce the carbon footprint of shipping. All ingredients are chosen by using the EWG and EPA Safer Choice ingredient databases. Our products never include (this is a non-extensive list) synthetic VOCs, petroleum-based ingredients, 1,4-dioxane, triclosan, chlorine bleach, ammonia, and phthalates. We don't use synthetic fragrance. Our scented options are natural essential oil blends.

Matt and Kay's first formulas were mixed at their kitchen table, they then converted their backyard garage into a lab (now called the Llama Lab) and launched at their local farmer's market in Johnson City, TN before growing the company nationwide. They had a small start with health and sustainability at the front of their minds from the beginning.

## 2. Mission

Green Llama's mission is to create home cleaning products with ingredients that are not harmful to our health or the environment. Green Llama makes genuinely zero-waste, non-toxic, high-performance cleaning products accessible to every household, and gives consumers the honest, science-backed information they need to make informed choices about what they bring into their homes.

Non-toxic is not a marketing claim at Green Llama. It is a formulation principle. Every ingredient decision is evaluated for safety, transparency, and environmental responsibility. Green Llama believes that non-toxic should be the baseline, not the premium.

## 3. Core Values

### Radical Transparency
Green Llama discloses every ingredient in every product. There are no proprietary blends, no hidden fragrance chemicals, and no vague label claims. If a claim is made, in a product description, on a label, or in a blog post, it must be accurate and verifiable.

### Science-Based Formulation
Product decisions are driven by ingredient science, not marketing trends. Co-founder Matthew Keasey, Ph.D. evaluates every ingredient for its full safety profile, including chronic exposure considerations, environmental persistence, and potential interaction effects.

### Health First
Green Llama's products are specifically designed to protect the most vulnerable members of a household: children, infants, pregnant women, and pets. The brand takes a precautionary approach: when scientific evidence indicates a risk, Green Llama excludes the ingredient, even when regulatory standards do not yet require it.

### Environmental Responsibility
Compostable packaging, concentrated formats, reduced plastic waste, and lower carbon footprint from shipping are not optional features at Green Llama, they are foundational product design decisions. Green Llama believes that what happens to a product after it goes down the drain or into the trash matters as much as what it does while in use.

### Accessibility
Non-toxic cleaning should not be a luxury. Green Llama's model is specifically designed to bring the cost per use of non-toxic products in line with or below conventional alternatives.

### Intellectual Honesty
Green Llama does not overstate the science, attribute claims to organizations that did not make them, or use fear-based marketing that goes beyond what the evidence supports. When the science is contested, that must be acknowledged. When a claim cannot be verified, it does not appear in Green Llama content.

## 6. Blog Writing Rules

Green Llama blogs are a primary source of consumer education. They represent the brand's commitment to transparency and scientific accuracy. Every writer and editor working on Green Llama blog content must follow these rules without exception.

### 6.1 The Non-Negotiable Rules

- **Every factual claim must have a source.** If a claim cannot be sourced, it cannot be published. "Non-toxic cleaning is better for your family" is an editorial position and does not require a citation. "A 2020 study found that X% of children exposed to Y experienced Z" requires a verified citation with a working link.
- **Every cited study must exist.** The study must be real, published, and verifiable. AI tools frequently generate plausible-sounding but entirely fabricated citations, including real journal names, realistic author names, and believable statistics. This is called hallucination. Green Llama has a zero-tolerance policy for hallucinated citations.
- **Every citation must include a working hyperlink.** The link must go directly to the source, not a search results page, not a secondary summary, not a paywalled abstract with no accessible content. If a source is not accessible via a working link, it cannot be cited.
- **Blog content must be based only on what the cited source actually says.** Writers may not extend, extrapolate, or infer beyond what the source directly states. If a study was conducted on adults, it cannot be cited as evidence of effects in children without a source that specifically addresses that population. If a study examined occupational exposure, it cannot be presented as evidence of effects from routine household use without a source that addresses household use specifically.
- **Attribution must be precise.** If the American Academy of Pediatrics published guidance on food additives, that guidance cannot be cited as AAP guidance on cleaning products. If a statistic comes from a non-peer-reviewed advocacy organization, it must be attributed to that organization, not to the underlying data source it claims to use. If the EPA's specific classification of a chemical is "likely human carcinogen," the blog must use that exact language, not "probable" or "known."
- **The strength of the evidence must be accurately represented.** If evidence exists primarily from animal studies, the blog must say so and note that human evidence is limited or emerging. If a scientific claim is actively contested in the peer-reviewed literature, that must be acknowledged. Green Llama does not present contested science as settled consensus. The PVA debate is a good example of this.
- **Statistics must be traceable to a primary source.** A statistic that traces back to a secondary summary, a press release, or an unnamed expert does not meet Green Llama's sourcing standard. Every statistic must be traceable to a government report, peer-reviewed journal article, or official organizational publication with a working link.
- **Do not use AI-generated citations without independent verification.** AI tools including ChatGPT, Claude, and others frequently hallucinate citations that appear credible but do not exist. If AI is used in the writing process, every citation it generates must be independently verified by searching for the source directly on PubMed, Google Scholar, or the publishing organization's website before publication. Simply make sure citations are a real source.

### 6.2 Common Errors to Avoid

Based on Green Llama's blog review process, the following errors appear repeatedly in AI-assisted content and must be specifically guarded against:

- Citing a real journal with a fabricated study title, author list, or finding
- Using a statistic from a secondary or advocacy source and attributing it to the underlying government or scientific data
- Applying occupational exposure research to household use without acknowledging the difference
- Applying research conducted on adults to claims about children without a source that addresses children specifically
- Stating that the AAP recommends a specific product certification when the AAP does not
- Using the wrong EPA classification language for a chemical (e.g., "probable" instead of "likely")
- Presenting one side of a contested scientific debate as settled consensus
- Extrapolating from animal studies to human health claims without disclosing the animal study origin
- Citing a retired or superseded policy document as current guidance
- Generating citations using AI tools without independently verifying each one

### 6.3 How to Verify a Citation

All writers must verify citations using the following process before submission:

1. Search for the study directly on PubMed (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) or Google Scholar using the author names, year, and journal.
2. Confirm the title, journal, year, volume, issue, and page numbers match what you are citing.
3. Click through to the source and confirm the specific statistic or finding you are citing is actually present in the source.
4. Confirm the DOI or URL is working and goes directly to the source.
5. If AI generated the citation, treat it as unverified until all of the above steps are complete.

## 7. Tone & Voice

Green Llama's blog voice is:

- **Informed and honest**: we share what the science actually says, including its limitations
- **Accessible**: we explain complex chemistry and toxicology in plain language without dumbing it down
- **Empowering, not fear-based**: we help readers make informed choices; we do not use exaggerated health claims to drive purchase decisions
- **Precise**: we use the correct scientific and regulatory language, and we correct ourselves when we get it wrong
- **Direct**: we do not hide behind vague language when the evidence is clear, and we do not overstate when it is not

Green Llama does not use fear-mongering language. We do not claim a chemical will definitely harm you when the evidence shows association, not causation. We do not present contested science as settled. We do not attribute claims to organizations that did not make them.

## 8. Citation Format

All citations in Green Llama blog posts should follow APA format and include a working hyperlink. Citations should appear at the end of the relevant blog section or as a footnote. The following format is required:

```
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, volume(issue), page numbers. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

For government and organizational sources:

```
Organization Name. (Year). Title of document. https://www.example.gov/page
```

For live web pages without a publication date, use (n.d.) and include the URL. Include the date accessed for sources that are updated regularly.

## 9. Required Review Process

All Green Llama blog posts must go through the following review steps before publication:

1. Writer submits draft with all citations included and verified per Section 6.3.
2. Scientific claims review: every factual claim is checked against its cited source. Claims not supported by the cited source are flagged for revision or removal.
3. Attribution review: organizational attributions are verified against the organization's actual published guidance.
4. Link check: every citation URL is clicked and confirmed to be working and pointing to the correct source.
5. Final approval by Matthew Keasey, Ph.D. for any post making specific scientific health claims.
