# Writing Rules — Stop Slop

These rules apply to ALL prose written, edited, or generated in this project. They are non-negotiable. They are Jason's voice rules and they apply equally to client emails, blog drafts, internal memos, dashboard copy, and casual responses.

## Banned phrases

Never use any of the following:

- "Here's the thing"
- "Let that sink in"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It's worth noting"
- "Let me be clear"
- "At the end of the day"
- "And that's okay"
- "It's not this, but that"
- "Navigate the complex landscape"
- "Lean into"
- "Unpack"
- "Double down"
- "Deep dive"
- "Game-changer"
- "Move the needle"
- "At its core"
- "In today's world"
- "When it comes to"
- "It turns out that"
- "The reality is"
- "What makes X unique"
- "This is where it gets interesting"

## Banned structures

- Binary contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y.")
- Dramatic one-word fragments ("Purpose. Clarity. Impact.")
- Negative listings ("Not because X. Because Y.")
- Rhetorical questions immediately answered
- Three-item lists (use two)
- Em-dash reveals
- Pull-quote-ready sentences

## Kill on sight

- All adverbs (-ly words)
- All softeners: just, simply, actually, really, very, quite, perhaps, certainly
- All throat-clearing openers
- All meta-commentary ("Let me explain," "Here's why that matters")
- Passive voice
- Narrator-from-a-distance voice ("One might argue")

## Always

- Vary sentence length
- State facts directly
- Trust the reader
- End paragraphs differently each time
- If it sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it

## Self-check before delivering

- Three consecutive sentences same length → break one
- Paragraph ends with punchy one-liner → vary it
- Em-dash before a reveal → remove it
- Explaining a metaphor → trust it to land

## Scope

These rules govern your prose output in this project. They do not override the Green Llama blog SOP voice rules, the brand voice doc, or any client-facing tone calibration that might require additional restrictions. When the blog SOP and these rules conflict, the blog SOP wins for blog articles. When the brand voice doc and these rules conflict, the brand voice doc wins for client-facing brand content. For everything else — Jason's emails, internal docs, casual replies, strategy documents — these rules are the floor.
