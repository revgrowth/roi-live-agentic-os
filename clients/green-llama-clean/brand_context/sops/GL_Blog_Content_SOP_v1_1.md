## CRITICAL RULES (Read First — Violations Require Full Rewrite)

### Rule 1: No Liquid Products — Ever
Green Llama does NOT make liquid products. Their entire business model eliminates liquid cleaning products. **Never** describe, imply, or depict Green Llama as making, selling, or recommending any liquid product — including liquid detergent, liquid soap, liquid refills, or liquid concentrate. The only spray products use reusable glass bottles with **dissolvable tablet** refills (not liquid refill pouches).

- Laundry product = "powder" or "concentrated powder" — never "pods," "sheets," or "liquid"
- Dish soap = "solid bar" or "coconut oil-based bar" — never "liquid dish soap"
- Surface cleaners = "refill tablets dissolved in water in a reusable glass bottle" — never "liquid refill"

### Rule 2: Zero Fabricated Data
Every statistic, study reference, and data point must be **real and verifiable**. AI tools (including Claude) frequently hallucinate plausible-sounding citations — real journal names, realistic authors, believable numbers — that do not exist. Green Llama's CSO (Ph.D. molecular neuroscientist) reviews every article.

**If you cannot verify a statistic in a Tier 1/2 source within 5 minutes, do not include it.** Write around the gap using general scientific principles, certification references, or qualitative statements. Use `[STAT NEEDED – verify before publish]` to flag gaps **during internal drafting only** — see Rule 6 for submission requirements.

### Rule 3: Every Citation Must Be Real
- Every cited study must exist as a real, published, verifiable work
- Every citation must include a working URL or DOI that goes directly to the source
- Every statistic must be traceable to a primary source (government report, peer-reviewed journal, official organizational publication)
- If AI generated a citation, treat it as **unverified** until confirmed via PubMed, Google Scholar, or the publisher's website

### Rule 4: Precision in Attribution
- If the AAP published guidance on food additives → you cannot cite it as AAP guidance on cleaning products
- If the EPA classifies a chemical as "likely human carcinogen" → write "likely" — not "probable" or "known"
- If a study used adult subjects → you cannot cite it as evidence of effects in children
- If research examined occupational exposure → you cannot present it as evidence of household use effects
- If evidence comes from animal studies → disclose this and note that human evidence is limited

### Rule 5: No Fear-Mongering
- Do not claim a chemical "will definitely harm you" when evidence shows association, not causation
- Do not present contested science as settled consensus (e.g., PVA biodegradability debate)
- Do not use exaggerated health claims to drive purchase decisions
- Do not make absolute safety claims ("completely safe for everyone")

### Rule 6: No Unresolved Placeholders in Submitted Drafts
`[STAT NEEDED]` tags are a drafting tool. They must **never** appear in a draft submitted to the client. Before submission, every placeholder must be resolved: either filled with verified data from a Tier 1/2 source, or the sentence rewritten to remove the need for the statistic. The client should never have to locate sources on our behalf. If a gap cannot be filled, rewrite the passage using general scientific principles, certification references, or qualitative statements grounded in known facts.

## SOURCE HIERARCHY & CITATION RULES

### Approved Source Tiers

| Tier | Source Types | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Preferred)** | Peer-reviewed journals, government databases | PubMed/NCBI, EPA.gov, EWG.org, FDA.gov, PubChem, ChemExpo Knowledgebase |
| **Tier 2 (Acceptable)** | University research, international bodies, standards organizations | .edu domains, WHO, OECD, established professional organizations |
| **Tier 3 (Use with caution)** | Major news outlets reporting on studies | NYT, Reuters — always trace back to the original study and cite that |
| **Never Acceptable** | AI summaries, unverifiable blogs, Wikipedia as primary source, social media | ChatGPT output, blog aggregators, Reddit, Facebook |

### Green Llama's Own Scientific Source Databases
- Environmental Working Group: https://www.ewg.org/
- EPA Safer Choice: https://www.epa.gov/saferchoice/safer-ingredients
- PubMed: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/
- ChemExpo Knowledgebase: https://comptox.epa.gov/chemexpo
- PubChem: https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/

### Citation Format (APA with Hyperlink)
Every article must include a "Sources" section at the bottom with full APA citations and working URLs.

**Journal article:**
```
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, volume(issue), page numbers. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

**Government/organizational source:**
```
Organization Name. (Year). Title of document. https://www.example.gov/page
```

**Web page without publication date:**
```
Organization Name. (n.d.). Title of page. https://www.example.com/page
```

### Citation Verification Checklist (mandatory for every citation)
1. Search for the study on PubMed or Google Scholar using author names, year, and journal
2. Confirm title, journal, year, volume, issue, and page numbers match
3. Click through to the source — confirm the specific statistic or finding is present in the source
4. Confirm the DOI or URL works and goes directly to the source
5. If AI generated the citation → treat as unverified until all above steps pass
6. For government/legislative sources → verify bill numbers, statute section numbers, and URLs against the actual legislative database or agency website (see below)

### Legislative & Regulatory Citation Rules
AI tools frequently generate plausible but incorrect government citations. These are harder to catch than fabricated journal articles because the bill names, statute numbers, and URL structures look legitimate.

**Mandatory verification steps for any legislative or regulatory citation:**
- Verify bill numbers against the actual legislative database (e.g., leginfo.legislature.ca.gov for California bills, congress.gov for federal legislation)
- Confirm statute section numbers match the enrolled version of the law (e.g., Health & Safety Code §108950, not §108945)
- Test every .gov URL immediately before submission — government sites restructure frequently and AI-generated .gov paths are often wrong
- For EPA chemical pages (IRIS, CompTox): navigate to the page manually through the EPA site rather than trusting an AI-generated URL path
- For FTC documents: verify the document URL loads and matches the cited rule or guidance — FTC link structures change across site redesigns

**Known incorrect patterns from past content:**
- ❌ Citing California AB-1743 instead of SB-258 for the Cleaning Product Right to Know Act
- ❌ Generating plausible IRIS chemical landing page URLs that return 404 (e.g., wrong substance_nmbr format)
- ❌ Using outdated FTC Green Guides URLs that no longer resolve

## KNOWN HALLUCINATION PATTERNS (Guard Against These)

These specific errors have appeared in past Green Llama content:

- Citing a real journal with a **fabricated** study title, author list, or finding
- Using a statistic from a secondary/advocacy source and attributing it to the underlying government data
- Applying occupational exposure research to household use without acknowledging the difference
- Applying adult research to claims about children without a source addressing children
- Stating the AAP recommends a specific product certification when the AAP does not
- Using the wrong EPA classification language (e.g., "probable" instead of "likely")
- Presenting one side of a contested debate as settled consensus (e.g., PVA biodegradability)
- Extrapolating from animal studies to human health claims without disclosing animal study origin
- Citing a retired or superseded policy document as current guidance
- Generating a plausible-sounding statistic that cannot be traced to any real source
- **Generating wrong legislative bill numbers** (e.g., citing California AB-1743 when the correct bill is SB-258) with wrong statute section numbers (§108945 instead of §108950)
- **Generating plausible but incorrect .gov URLs** — especially EPA IRIS chemical pages, FTC document links, and state legislative database URLs that return 404 or redirect to the wrong page
- **Leaving `[STAT NEEDED]` placeholders in submitted drafts** — treating the placeholder as a finished product rather than resolving it before submission

**Specific past fabrications flagged by the client:**
- ❌ "A lifecycle analysis published in the Journal of Industrial Ecology found that transportation accounted for 10 to 15% of the total environmental impact of liquid household cleaning products." → This study does not exist.
- ❌ "Americans throw away an estimated 2.5 billion plastic cleaning bottles every year, according to data from the EPA's municipal solid waste reports." → This statistic cannot be verified in EPA reports.

**Specific citation errors flagged in April 2026 article QA:**
- ❌ Cited California AB-1743 with §108945 → Correct bill is SB-258, correct section is §108950
- ❌ Generated FTC Green Guides URL that returned 404 → Correct URL required manual lookup on FTC site
- ❌ Generated EPA IRIS 1,4-dioxane URL with wrong path format → Correct URL required navigating IRIS manually
- ❌ Left two `[STAT NEEDED]` tags in submitted draft → Client had to locate PubMed studies (PMIDs 35642859, 22991565) and fill gaps herself

## ARTICLE STRUCTURE

### Standard Architecture (every article follows this)

```
1. H1 TITLE (with primary keyword)
2. BYLINE: By Kay Baker, MS, OTR/L | Reviewed by Matthew Keasey, Ph.D.
3. SEPARATOR (---)
4. BLUF PARAGRAPH (2–4 sentences answering the title question directly — this is the featured snippet / AEO candidate)
5. SEPARATOR (---)
6. H2 BODY SECTIONS (keyword-aligned headings, written as questions or direct statements matching search intent)
   - H3 SUBSECTIONS as needed
   - Internal links woven naturally into body text
   - Product mentions where organically relevant (correct names, correct formats)
7. H2 FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (3–6 FAQs, each answer 2–4 sentences, self-sufficient)
8. SOURCES SECTION (APA format, working URLs)
9. TRANSPARENCY NOTE: "Educational guide under Green Llama's EEAT & Trust Framework. Not medical or legal advice. Always follow product labels and spot-test first; store products away from children and pets."
10. RELATED READING (3–5 linked GL articles)
```

### BLUF Paragraph Rules
- Answers the article's core question in the first 2–4 sentences
- Direct, factual, no throat-clearing
- Primary candidate for featured snippet extraction and AI answer engine responses
- Include the most important certification or safety context
- Example from published article: "Non-toxic laundry detergent is a cleaning product formulated without ingredients that are known or suspected to cause harm to human health through normal use and exposure. This means the formula excludes chemicals linked to cancer (like 1,4-dioxane), endocrine disruption (like phthalates found in synthetic fragrances), respiratory irritation, and skin sensitization."

### AEO Optimization (Answer Engine Optimization)
- Open every article with a direct, factual BLUF answer
- Use definition-style sentences: "[Term] is [definition]."
- Structure FAQ answers as standalone, self-sufficient responses
- Include comparison structures: "Unlike [conventional approach], [Green Llama approach] does X because Y."
- Cite sources inline ("According to EPA data...") so AI engines can attribute claims

## VOICE & TONE

### Blog Voice Profile
- **Informed and honest** — share what the science says, including limitations
- **Accessible** — explain chemistry and toxicology in plain language at Grade 6–8 reading level without losing accuracy
- **Empowering, not fear-based** — help readers make informed choices; state risks proportionally
- **Precise** — use correct scientific and regulatory language
- **Direct** — do not hide behind vague language when evidence is clear; do not overstate when it is not

### Voice Calibration by Content Type

| Content Type | Authority | Warmth | Technical Depth | Product Integration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ingredient science / toxicology | Maximum | Medium | High — cite specific studies, mechanisms | Light — mention GL approach |
| Product comparison | High | Medium-High | Medium — specific claims with test data | Direct — fair comparison, acknowledge limitations |
| Family/baby safety | High (Kay's OT lens) | Maximum | Medium — translate science to practical advice | Moderate — recommend by name |
| How-to / cleaning guide | Medium | Maximum | Low-Medium — focus on practical steps | Moderate — where natural |
| Sustainability / environmental | High | Medium | High — LCA data, OECD standards | Moderate — packaging, format advantages |

### Green Llama Blog Content NEVER Does
- Uses fear-mongering language or scare tactics
- Claims a chemical will "definitely harm you" when evidence shows association, not causation
- Presents contested science as settled consensus
- Attributes claims to organizations that did not make them
- Overstates the strength of a single study
- Uses "toxic" without specifying what it means in context
- Makes absolute safety claims ("completely safe for everyone")
- Uses dramatic one-word fragments, binary contrasts, or pull-quote-ready sentences
- Uses adverbs, softeners, or throat-clearing openers

### Writing Rules (Style)
- Vary sentence length
- State facts directly
- Trust the reader
- End paragraphs differently each time
- No consecutive sentences of the same length
- No em-dash reveals
- No meta-commentary ("Let me explain," "Here's why that matters")
- Active voice throughout
- If it sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it

## ARTICLE PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

### Stage 1: Assignment & Research
- Receive assignment from topical map (title, primary keyword, cluster, target intent)
- Identify 2–4 secondary keywords and related search queries
- Confirm which GL products are relevant; verify names, formats, certifications, pricing on greenllamaclean.com
- Research using Tier 1/2 sources — collect 5–15 real, verified sources before writing
- Identify 3–8 existing GL articles to link to

### Stage 2: Outline & Source Mapping
- Create H2/H3 outline following standard architecture
- Map each major claim to a specific, verified source before writing prose
- Flag unsourced sections with `[STAT NEEDED]`
- Plan internal link and product mention placement

### Stage 3: Draft
- Write BLUF paragraph first
- Write body sections — every factual claim must reference a source from Stage 1
- Integrate internal links naturally
- Mention GL products with correct names and formats where organic
- Write FAQ section (3–6 Qs, 2–4 sentence answers)
- Compile Sources in APA format with working URLs
- Add Related Reading (3–5 articles)
- Add transparency note

### Stage 4: Source Verification Audit ⚠️ HARD GATE — No draft passes this stage with failures
**This stage is not optional. It is the single most common point of failure.** The first article produced under this SOP shipped with 3 broken URLs, 1 wrong bill number, and 2 unresolved `[STAT NEEDED]` placeholders. All were caught by the client. Every issue below must be resolved before the draft moves to Stage 5.

- Open every URL in Sources in a browser — confirm it loads and displays the correct source (not a 404, not a redirect to a search page, not a different document)
- For .gov URLs: navigate to the page through the agency's site navigation to confirm the URL is current — government sites restructure frequently
- For legislative citations: verify bill number and statute section against the actual legislative database
- Confirm each cited statistic exists in the linked source — read the relevant section, not the abstract alone
- Confirm author names, year, journal, title match the actual publication
- Remove or replace any citation that fails verification
- Resolve every `[STAT NEEDED]` placeholder — either fill with verified data or rewrite the sentence without the statistic. **Zero placeholders may remain in the draft.**
- Record verification results: for each source, note "URL confirmed working [date]" in your working notes

### Stage 5: Product & Brand Check
- No liquid product references anywhere
- All product names match greenllamaclean.com
- Product formats correct (powder, tablet, bar)
- Only current certifications referenced
- No fabricated pricing/availability/partnerships
- Voice matches profile: informed, accessible, empowering, precise, direct

### Stage 6: Submit to Green Llama
**Pre-submission gate** (verify all before uploading):
- Zero `[STAT NEEDED]` or other placeholder tags in the document
- Every URL in Sources section confirmed working within the past 24 hours
- All legislative bill numbers and statute sections verified against primary databases
- Pre-submission checklist (below) completed with all boxes checked

Draft submitted in CMS (Shopify blog, visibility = Hidden). Kay and Matt review. Their edits take priority.

### Stage 7: Publish
After GL approval: publish with correct author attribution, meta description, tags, featured image. Confirm all internal links working.
