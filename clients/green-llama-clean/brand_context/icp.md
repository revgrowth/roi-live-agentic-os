# Green Llama Clean — ICP

> Per the GLC voice approach, segments are defined by need state and mindset, not demographic categories alone. Demographics remain useful for media targeting; the segments below describe the buying logic.

## Primary segment — Health-first parents

**Who they are.** Parents (skewing toward mothers) of young children or infants. Often pet owners. Live in households where chemical exposure is a daily concern they actively manage. Frequently college-educated, household incomes $75K+, but the segment extends across income levels because health concerns are not income-bounded.

**What they're looking for.** Products that are demonstrably safer for children and pets without sacrificing cleaning effectiveness. They do their homework: read EWG ratings, check ingredient lists, ask in parenting communities, follow eco-clean creators on Instagram and TikTok.

**Buying logic.** Trust before convenience. Will switch brands for credible certification (EWG Verified is the strongest cue). Will pay a small premium for verifiable ingredient transparency, but will not pay a luxury premium. Subscription-friendly once trust is established.

**Triggers.** New baby, new pet, family member diagnosed with asthma or chemical sensitivity, news cycle on a specific household chemical, recommendation from a trusted source.

**Voice notes.** Kay Baker's occupational-therapist + parent voice resonates here. Matt's chemistry voice provides the credibility floor.

## Secondary segment — Eco-conscious lifestyle buyers

**Who they are.** Adults across age ranges (20s to 50s) who have made sustainability a stable part of their consumer behavior. Reusable bottles, compostable packaging, secondhand shopping, plant-forward eating. Plastic waste is a personal moral concern.

**What they're looking for.** Products that align with their values across the full lifecycle: manufacturing, packaging, use, disposal. Less focused on health risk; more focused on environmental footprint.

**Buying logic.** Values-aligned. Will pay a premium for compostable packaging, refill systems, and demonstrable carbon-footprint reduction. Skeptical of greenwashing. Third-party certifications matter.

**Triggers.** Plastic-waste content, refill-system messaging, B Corp / women-owned / cruelty-free signals.

**Voice notes.** Lean into the no-liquid model, the compostable packaging, the Tennessee local-manufacturing story. Underplay clinical health framing here.

## Tertiary segment — Sensitivity and health-condition driven

**Who they are.** Adults with diagnosed chemical sensitivities, asthma, eczema, contact dermatitis, or similar conditions where conventional cleaning products are documented triggers. Also caregivers for someone in this state.

**What they're looking for.** Specific ingredient exclusions confirmed in writing. Fragrance-free options. Products that have not caused reactions for others in their condition community.

**Buying logic.** Cautious sampling. Will buy small quantities first, escalate to full replacement if no reaction. Word-of-mouth and condition-community recommendations carry disproportionate weight.

**Triggers.** Reaction to a current product. Doctor or dermatologist recommendation. Condition-community thread on Reddit, Facebook groups, or condition-specific forums.

**Voice notes.** Dr. Beth Peterknecht's dermatology byline applies here. Be specific about what the formula EXCLUDES (fragrance, optical brighteners, dyes, preservatives). That's the language this segment searches in.

## Cross-segment language

All three segments respond to:

- "Plant- and mineral-based"
- "EWG Verified" (when applicable to the specific product)
- "Free from [specific named chemicals]" — name 1,4-dioxane, phthalates, synthetic fragrance, optical brighteners
- "Concentrated" / "refillable" / "compostable"
- Founder credentials (Kay's OT, Matt's Ph.D.)
- "Non-toxic" as a formulation principle, not a marketing claim

All three segments distrust:

- Vague eco-claims without certification
- Ingredient lists with proprietary blends
- Fear-based marketing without evidence
- Pricing that reads as luxury rather than accessible

## Out of scope

GLC content does not target:

- Hardcore zero-waste maximalists alone (overlap exists with Secondary; not the primary audience)
- Industrial / commercial cleaning buyers (different SKU and pricing logic)
- Pure price-shoppers (GLC's price band sits at conventional product cost, not below)
