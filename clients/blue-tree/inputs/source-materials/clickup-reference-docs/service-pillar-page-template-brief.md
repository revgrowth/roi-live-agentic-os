# Design Template Brief
## Service Pillar Page
### Blue Tree Landscaping
**Prepared by:** Jason Spencer | [ROI.LIVE](http://ROI.LIVE)
**For:** Creative Design Team **Date:** February 2026 **Reference Page:** `/landscapes/` — Landscape Design & Installation
* * *
## What This Template Is
The Service Pillar Page is the most important page type in the site architecture after the homepage. There are exactly **5 of these pages** — one for each service pillar:
*   `/landscapes/`
*   `/hardscapes/`
*   `/pools/`
*   `/healthy-yard/`
*   `/premier-outdoor-services/`
Each pillar page is the **topical authority hub** for its service category. It answers the broadest version of the question — "Who does landscaping in Southeastern PA?" — and then systematically builds the case for Blue Tree before pointing visitors to more specific cluster pages beneath it.
Think of it as the **full brand experience for a service category.** It has to do three things simultaneously: establish authority, build trust, and convert. It's a long page that earns the scroll.
* * *
## Page Personality
**Length:** Long. 3,000–4,000 words of visible content. Designed to be read in full by serious prospects who are in research mode, and to be skimmed by visitors who are further along and just want to find the CTA.
**Tone:** Premium residential. Warm but confident. This is a high-ticket service sold to affluent homeowners in Southeastern PA — the design should feel like a firm handshake, not a sales pitch. Think architectural firm meets trusted neighbor.
**Scroll behavior:** The page should reward the scroll. Each section should feel like a natural next step in a conversation — not a wall of text, not a brochure dump. The visitor should feel increasingly confident in Blue Tree as they move down the page.
* * *
## Section-by-Section Layout Specification
* * *
### 1\. HERO SECTION
**Purpose:** Establish category authority and capture the CTA before the visitor reads a word.
**Layout:**
*   Full-viewport height (100vh) on desktop, minimum 80vh on mobile
*   Full-bleed background: high-quality photography of a completed project in this service category. Real photography only — no stock. Consider an auto-playing muted video loop as an option for desktop.
*   Dark overlay (gradient from bottom) for text legibility
*   Content is left-aligned, positioned in the lower-left quadrant of the hero on desktop. Centered on mobile.
**Content stack (top to bottom):**
1. Breadcrumb navigation — small, subtle, above the headline (`Home > Landscapes`)
2. Pre-headline trust badge — one line, small caps or label style, high contrast (`Degreed Designers. 40+ Years Local Experience. Plants That Actually Thrive Here.`)
3. H1 headline — large, commanding. This is the biggest type on the page.
4. Subheadline — 1–2 sentences, 18–20px, moderate weight. Expands on the H1.
5. CTA button cluster — Primary button (filled, brand color) + Secondary CTA (ghost/outline or text link)
6. Trust bar — horizontal row of 4 trust signals with checkmarks or small icons. Sits at the bottom of the hero content, above the fold line.
**Design notes:**
*   The H1 should be the largest text element on the entire page. Set the hierarchy from here.
*   Primary CTA button must be visible without scrolling on all breakpoints.
*   Trust bar can sit flush against the bottom of the hero or just below it — it should feel anchored, not floating.
*   On mobile, stack CTAs vertically. Reduce hero height. Keep trust bar as a horizontal scroll or 2x2 grid.
* * *
### 2\. INTRODUCTION SECTION
**Purpose:** Hook the visitor with a problem/empathy statement before presenting Blue Tree as the solution.
**Layout:**
*   Constrained content width (max 800px centered or left-aligned within a wider container)
*   Text-heavy section — this is intentional. It's a reading section, not a visual section.
*   Single pull-quote callout box, visually distinguished (background color, left border accent, or card treatment)
**Design notes:**
*   No competing visuals here. The copy does the work. Give it breathing room.
*   The callout box should feel like a signed design philosophy statement — authoritative, not decorative.
*   Consider a subtle background texture or color shift to differentiate this section from the hero without a hard break.
* * *
### 3\. PERSONAL PROMISE SECTION
**Purpose:** Create a human moment. This is owner-voice content from Jeff & Chad — it should feel personal and warm, not corporate.
**Layout:**
*   Two-column on desktop: owner photo (or photo of both owners) on one side, promise content on the other. Offset layout preferred — photo slightly overlapping the section boundary above or below.
*   Single column on mobile, photo above content.
*   Three promise statements styled as bold lead-ins followed by paragraph copy. Not a bulleted list — flowing text with visual emphasis on the opening phrase of each promise.
**Design notes:**
*   This section should have the warmest visual treatment on the page. Consider a slightly warmer background tone, softer shadows, or a texture that feels residential rather than corporate.
*   If owner signatures are available, use them instead of or in addition to the photo. Signatures add authenticity.
*   This section should NOT look like a features grid. It's a letter, not a spec sheet.
* * *
### 4\. DESIGN APPROACH SECTION
**Purpose:** Establish credibility through process and technology. Show that Blue Tree designs with expertise, not guesswork.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline, full width
*   Three-column feature block on desktop, single column on mobile
*   Each column: icon (top) → short bold headline → 2–3 sentence description
*   CTA link below the three columns ("Meet Our Design Team →")
**Design notes:**
*   Icons should be consistent in style — either all line icons or all filled. No mixing.
*   This is a confidence-building section, not a wow moment. Clean, structured, professional.
*   Consider a light background differentiation (subtle gray or off-white) to visually separate this from adjacent sections.
* * *
### 5\. PLANNING QUESTIONS SECTION
**Purpose:** Self-qualification. Helps the visitor see themselves in the process and primes them for the consultation CTA.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   2x4 grid on desktop (2 columns, 4 rows), 1x8 on mobile
*   Each cell: small icon + one question. Compact cards with subtle border or background.
*   Callout note below the grid ("Don't worry if you don't have all the answers...")
*   CTA below the callout
**Design notes:**
*   This section should feel interactive and approachable — not like a form, but like a thoughtful checklist.
*   Cards can have a light hover state on desktop (subtle lift or border color change).
*   Keep the grid tight. This is not a hero moment — it's a transitional section that moves the visitor toward the consultation CTA.
* * *
### 6\. SERVICES GRID SECTION
**Purpose:** Show the breadth of what's available within this service category. Each card links to a cluster page.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   3x2 grid on desktop (3 columns, 2 rows), 2x3 on tablet, 1x6 on mobile
*   Each card: icon (top) → service headline → 2–3 sentence description → "Learn More →" link
*   Cards should have consistent height with the link anchored to the bottom of the card
*   CTA below the grid ("Explore All Landscaping Services →")
**Design notes:**
*   Cards link to cluster pages. Cards whose cluster pages aren't yet live should render without the link — visually identical, but the "Learn More →" link is absent or greyed out. Do NOT link to unpublished pages.
*   Consider a hover state that reveals more of the card or lifts it slightly — these cards are meant to be explored.
*   Photography inside cards is optional but powerful if available. Service-specific photos in each card thumbnail elevate the section significantly.
* * *
### 7\. LOCAL EXPERTISE SECTION
**Purpose:** Differentiate Blue Tree on hyper-local knowledge. Combat the perception that "any landscaper can do this."
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   Body copy block (3–4 paragraphs) — constrained width, reading column
*   Four callout blocks below the body copy: arranged in a 4-column row on desktop, 2x2 on tablet, stacked on mobile
*   Each callout: icon → short bold headline → one sentence
**Design notes:**
*   Consider using a map or illustrated PA county graphic as a background element or sidebar visual.
*   The four callout blocks should be compact and punchy — these are facts, not features. Keep them tight.
*   This section can use a background image (aerial of a Southeastern PA property or landscape) with an overlay for texture without sacrificing legibility.
* * *
### 8\. PROCESS SECTION
**Purpose:** Reduce friction by making the path to hire feel clear and manageable.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   Five numbered steps in a horizontal flow on desktop (connected by a line or arrow), vertical timeline on mobile
*   Each step: step number (large, styled) → bold headline → 2–3 sentence description
*   Post-installation callout box below the steps — visually differentiated (card with accent border or background)
*   CTA below the callout
**Design notes:**
*   The connecting line between steps should feel like a journey, not a flowchart. Consider a curved or organic line rather than a straight arrow.
*   Step numbers should be large and confident — they anchor the section visually.
*   The post-installation callout ("We Don't Disappear After Planting") is a trust-builder and should feel warmer than the process steps — consider a softer background or a green plant-adjacent color.
*   On mobile, the vertical timeline should have clear visual separation between steps. Numbered markers on the left, content on the right.
* * *
### 9\. PORTFOLIO SECTION
**Purpose:** Visual proof. Show the work, not just the words.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   Masonry or uniform grid of 4–6 project thumbnails
*   Each thumbnail: hover overlay showing project type + location
*   Filter bar above the grid (Operation Tag filter — shows "Landscapes" content by default)
*   CTA below the grid ("View Full Landscape Portfolio →")
**Design notes:**
*   Photography quality is everything here. If the photos aren't exceptional, reduce the grid to 4 strong images rather than showing 6 mediocre ones.
*   Hover overlays should be smooth and legible — dark overlay with white text.
*   Lightbox on click — opens full image with project details.
*   Lazy load all images for performance.
*   On mobile, 2-column grid. No masonry on mobile — uniform height is cleaner.
* * *
### 10\. VIDEO TESTIMONIALS SECTION
**Purpose:** Social proof through faces and voices. The most trusted form of testimonial.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   3–4 video thumbnails in a horizontal row on desktop, 2-column grid on tablet, single column on mobile
*   Each thumbnail: project still image → play button overlay → client name + project type below
*   CTA below ("Watch More Project Videos →")
**Design notes:**
*   Thumbnails should look like editorial photography, not screen captures. Use a frame from the video that shows the finished project, not the homeowner talking.
*   Play button should be unmistakable but not garish — consider a semi-transparent circle with a clean triangle.
*   Videos do NOT autoplay. Ever. Not even muted.
*   If fewer than 3 videos are available at launch, use 2 videos + 1 placeholder with "Coming Soon" treatment or omit the section until content is ready.
* * *
### 11\. WHY BLUE TREE SECTION
**Purpose:** Directly address the comparison question. Why Blue Tree over someone cheaper?
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   3x2 grid of value blocks on desktop (3 columns, 2 rows), 2x3 on tablet, stacked on mobile
*   Each block: icon → bold headline → 2–3 sentence description
*   No CTA — this section flows directly into the Integration section
**Design notes:**
*   This section answers an objection, so the tone should feel confident and direct — not defensive.
*   Consider a darker or contrasting background for this section to give it visual weight and separate it from adjacent sections.
*   Icons should reinforce the headline concept, not be generic checkmarks.
* * *
### 12\. INTEGRATION SECTION
**Purpose:** Show that Blue Tree is a full-service outdoor living company — not just a landscaper.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   Body copy block (2–3 paragraphs) — reading column, constrained width
*   Five related service cards below the body copy — horizontal row on desktop, 2-column grid on tablet, stacked on mobile
*   Each card: service name → one sentence description → "Explore \[Service\] →" link
**Design notes:**
*   Related service cards link to OTHER pillar pages and cross-cluster pages. They should be visually lighter than the Services Grid cards earlier in the page — these are navigation aids, not primary service features.
*   Consider a simple card style with a left accent line or small icon — clean and directive, not competing with the main service grid.
*   This section is the "and there's more" moment — don't overdesign it.
* * *
### 13\. TESTIMONIALS SECTION
**Purpose:** Peer validation from real clients.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline
*   Three testimonials displayed as large quote cards — horizontal row on desktop, stacked on mobile
*   Each card: large opening quotation mark (decorative) → quote text → client attribution (name + location)
*   CTA below ("Read More Reviews →")
**Design notes:**
*   Large decorative quotation marks in brand color or a muted tone.
*   Client attribution should include location — this reinforces geographic relevance.
*   If photos of completed projects are available alongside testimonials, use them. A before/after or project photo next to the quote is more compelling than a quote alone.
*   On mobile, consider a swipe carousel rather than stacked cards.
* * *
### 14\. SERVICE AREA SECTION
**Purpose:** Confirm geographic coverage and link to location-specific pages.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline + subheadline
*   Map visual (interactive or high-quality static) showing service area counties
*   County name list below or beside the map
*   Featured communities list (towns) — inline text links
*   Three location-specific CTA links (e.g., "Landscaping in Blue Bell →")
*   CTA button ("Find Your Area →")
**Design notes:**
*   The map is the hero of this section. Use an actual rendered map of Southeastern PA with the service counties highlighted — not a generic US map.
*   County and town names should be scannable. Use a clean typographic list or inline link treatment.
*   This section should feel grounding and confident — "we know your backyard because we've worked in your neighborhood."
* * *
### 15\. FAQ SECTION
**Purpose:** Answer objections, target AEO/featured snippet queries, and keep visitors on the page longer.
**Layout:**
*   Section headline
*   10 FAQ items in accordion style — all collapsed by default, smooth expand/collapse animation
*   Each item: question as the trigger (bold, full-width clickable row with +/− or chevron indicator) → answer revealed on click
*   CTA below the accordion ("Have More Questions? Contact Us →")
**Design notes:**
*   Accordion items should have clear visual separation — a bottom border or subtle background on the trigger row.
*   Answers can contain inline text links to cluster pages — these should be styled as standard in-text links, not buttons.
*   All 10 FAQs should be present in the DOM (not hidden behind "Load More") for schema markup and indexing.
*   On mobile, the accordion is already the right pattern — no changes needed.
* * *
### 16\. FINAL CTA SECTION
**Purpose:** The last conversion opportunity. Capture visitors who have read the whole page.
**Layout:**
*   Full-width section, visually distinct from everything above — consider a dark background, a brand color fill, or a full-bleed photography background
*   Section headline (large) + subheadline
*   Short body copy paragraph (3–4 sentences)
*   Primary CTA button (large, high contrast)
*   Secondary CTA (phone number as tap-to-call link)
*   Trust reinforcement strip below the CTAs (4 trust signals: No Obligation, Free Consultation, NALP Affiliated, 1-Year Warranty)
**Design notes:**
*   This section should feel like a landing. The visual treatment should shift enough that the visitor knows they've reached a decision point.
*   The primary CTA button should be the largest, most prominent button on the entire page.
*   Trust signals at the bottom of this section echo the hero trust bar — bookending the page with confidence signals.
* * *
## Sticky Elements
**Sticky Header:**
*   Appears after the hero scrolls out of view
*   Contains: Logo (left) + primary navigation (center) + phone number + "Request Consultation" CTA button (right)
*   Should be slim and unobtrusive — not a second hero
**Mobile Fixed CTA:**
*   A tap-to-call button or "Request Consultation" button fixed to the bottom of the viewport on mobile
*   Should not obscure content — use appropriate bottom padding on the page content

## Performance Requirements
*   All images lazy-loaded below the fold
*   Hero image/video optimized and preloaded
*   LCP target: under 2.5 seconds
*   No layout shift from font loading (use font-display: swap)
*   Video thumbnails are static images until clicked — no video preload
* * *
## Schema & SEO Notes for Developers
*   BreadcrumbList schema: `Home > [Pillar Name]`
*   Service schema with LocalBusiness nested provider
*   FAQPage schema on all 10 accordion items
*   All cluster page links must be crawlable anchor tags — not JavaScript-rendered
*   Canonical tag: self-referencing
*   OG image: hero photography cropped to 1200x630
