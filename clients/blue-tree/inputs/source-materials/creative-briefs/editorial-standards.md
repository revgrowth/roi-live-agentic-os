## IMPORTANT: THIS IS A LIGHTWEIGHT TRUST PAGE
This page is **not** a content-heavy pillar or conversion page. It is a concise (300–500 words) E-E-A-T trust declaration that documents Blue Tree's editorial process for blog and resource content. Its purpose is to be **linked from every blog post** via the editorial standards statement, providing Google Quality Raters and AI answer engines with a verifiable signal of content integrity.
Think of this page as the "editorial policy" disclosure that major publications maintain — a short, clear, professional statement that says: _Here is how we create content, who creates it, and how we ensure it is accurate._ It does not need to sell anything. It needs to instill confidence.
**Why it matters for SEO and AEO:**
1. **Google Quality Raters.** The Quality Rater Guidelines specifically look for editorial standards disclosures when evaluating content trustworthiness. Every blog post on the site links to this page from its editorial standards statement — creating a site-wide trust signal.
2. **AI Answer Engines.** When an LLM evaluates whether to cite Blue Tree's content, it looks for signals of editorial rigor — author credentials, review processes, fact-checking methodology. This page consolidates those signals into a single, crawlable, citable document.
3. **E-E-A-T Chain Completion.** The editorial standards page is the final link in the trust chain: Blog Post → Author Byline → Author Bio Page → Editorial Standards. Without this page, the chain is incomplete.

## 1.1 Page Purpose
The Editorial Standards page exists to answer one question: **"How does Blue Tree ensure the accuracy and quality of its published content?"**
This page is not written for homeowners shopping for a pool estimate. It is written for three specific audiences who directly influence Blue Tree's search visibility and AI citation eligibility:
**1\. Google Quality Raters** evaluate content trustworthiness as part of Google's search quality process. The Quality Rater Guidelines specifically reference editorial standards as a trust signal for websites that publish informational content. Every blog post on the Blue Tree site links to this page — meaning every blog post carries a visible trust signal that Quality Raters can verify.
**2\. AI Answer Engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, etc.)** decide which sources to cite based on content authority signals. An editorial standards page — with named authors, documented review processes, and stated accuracy commitments — makes Blue Tree's content significantly more likely to be cited as a trusted source in AI-generated answers about pools, landscaping, and outdoor living in Southeastern PA.
**3\. Discerning homeowners** who click the "Editorial Standards" link at the bottom of a blog post. These visitors are already reading Blue Tree's content — they want reassurance that the information is reliable before making decisions based on it.
This page is a trust investment, not a traffic driver. It will not rank for commercial keywords. Its value is measured by what it does for every _other_ page that links to it.

## 1.4 Content Strategy
This is a 300–500 word page. Every sentence must earn its place. The content strategy follows four principles:
1. **Name the people.** Anonymous editorial standards are worthless. This page names the types of professionals who write Blue Tree's content and links to their individual bio pages.
2. **Describe the process.** A clear, honest description of how content moves from draft to publication — including the dual-author review system (writer + reviewer).
3. **State the accuracy commitment.** Explicit language about fact-checking against Blue Tree's own project data, manufacturer specifications, and authoritative external sources.
4. **Commit to freshness.** A stated cadence for reviewing and updating published content — because stale content loses trust with both search engines and AI systems.

## 2.4 Section 3: Our Editorial Process
**H2:** How We Create and Review Content
**Copy:**
> Our editorial process is built around a simple principle: every piece of published content should be accurate enough that we would use it to make decisions on our own projects.  
> **Qualified authorship.** Each article is written by a team member whose professional experience directly relates to the topic. Pool content is authored by our pool construction and design professionals. Landscape and garden content is written by our degreed designers. Turf care and lawn health content is authored by our turfcare management team. This is not general knowledge — it is practitioner-level expertise from professionals who do this work every day across Southeastern PA.  
> **Peer review.** Before publication, every article is reviewed by a second Blue Tree team member for technical accuracy. The reviewer is identified by name alongside the author on each published article — so you always know who wrote it and who checked it.  
> **Verified sources.** Where we reference cost ranges, material specifications, or technical data, we draw from our own project records and verified external sources — including manufacturer documentation, Penn State Extension resources, and industry organizations such as NALP (National Association of Landscape Professionals).  
> **Content freshness.** We review published articles at least annually to ensure accuracy — and more frequently for content that includes pricing, seasonal recommendations, or information affected by code and regulation changes. Every article displays a "Last Updated" date so you can see when it was most recently reviewed.
**Word Count:** ~210 words
> **Design Direction:** The H2 introduces this section. The four sub-points (Qualified authorship, Peer review, Verified sources, Content freshness) use **bold lead-in phrases** followed by paragraph prose — not bullet points, not numbered lists. Each sub-point is a standard paragraph with the first two to three words bolded as a scannable label. This keeps the page feeling editorial rather than listy, while still providing clear structure for both human readers and AI extraction.  
> **AEO Note:** The first sentence of each sub-point is written in simple, declarative structure optimized for AI extraction. These four statements are the answer capsule targets for this page.
