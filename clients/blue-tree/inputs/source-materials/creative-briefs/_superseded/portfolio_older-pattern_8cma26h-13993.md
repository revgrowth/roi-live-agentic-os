# PART 1 — PAGE STRATEGY

## 1.1 Page Purpose

This is the **visual proof engine** for the entire Blue Tree website. Every service claim made on every pillar page, every cluster page, every location page, and every blog post ultimately points back here for evidence. When a prospect asks "Can they actually do what they say they can do?" — this page answers with undeniable photographic proof, project scope details, and client stories.

The Completed Projects page serves a dual role:

1. **Hub Page** — The index/listing view at `/portfolio/completed-projects/` that aggregates all case studies, filterable by Operation Tag (Pools, Landscapes, Hardscapes, Healthy Yard, Premier Outdoor Services) and by location.
2. **Individual Case Study Template** — Each featured project gets a dedicated child page (e.g., `/portfolio/completed-projects/pools/montgomery-county/blue-bell/name-of-the-project`) that functions as a standalone visual proof asset that can be linked from service pages, location pages, blog posts, and shared on social media.

This brief covers **both** the hub page design and the individual case study page template.

## 1.2 Conversion Goals

| Priority | Goal | Metric |
| ---| ---| --- |
| 1 | Convert project-browsing visitors into consultation requests | CTA clicks → `/request-estimate/` from case study pages |
| 2 | Supply social proof to service and location pages via internal links | Inbound link referrals from portfolio → service pages |
| 3 | Generate social media engagement and referral traffic | Social share clicks per project, referral sessions from social |
| 4 | Provide AI-citable evidence of Blue Tree's completed work | AI citation rate when answering "Blue Tree projects," "Blue Tree reviews," "pool builders near \[town\]" |
| 5 | Reduce sales cycle by pre-qualifying prospects on project scope | Time-on-page, scroll depth, pages per session after portfolio entry |

## 1.3 Target Audience

This page is visited by people in two distinct stages:

**Visual Validation Stage** (highest volume):

*   Prospects who have seen a service page and want proof before committing
*   Homeowners comparing Blue Tree's portfolio quality against competitors
*   Visitors sent here by a designer during the proposal stage ("check out our past work")
*   Spouses or co-decision-makers reviewing the company after the first consultation

**Inspiration Stage** (high engagement):

*   Homeowners in early planning who are browsing for ideas before they know what they want
*   People searching "\[service\] ideas" or "backyard transformation" who land on case studies via organic search or image search
*   Social media visitors who clicked through from a shared project photo

## 1.4 Conversion Psychology

These visitors are in **show-me mode**. They're done reading about what Blue Tree can do — they want to see it. The page must:

*   **Lead with the work** — photos first, copy second. This is not a text page with images; it's an image page with supporting context.
*   **Create aspiration** — "I want my backyard to look like that."
*   **Demonstrate range** — From straightforward paver patios to resort-style pool complexes, the portfolio must show Blue Tree can handle their specific project type.
*   **Reduce scope anxiety** — Showing the progression from bare yard to finished project makes large projects feel manageable.
*   **Trigger "they're in my area" recognition** — Town names in case study titles create geographic proximity trust.
*   **Offer the next step at peak desire** — CTA appears when the prospect is most emotionally invested in the visuals.

### B.4 The Story Section

**H2:** The Vision

**Copy Framework:**

This section tells the project story in 3 beats:

**Beat 1 — The Starting Point:**
What was the homeowner's situation? What did the yard look like before? What problem were they trying to solve, or what experience were they trying to create?

> _Example copy:_ "When the \[family name or 'homeowners'\] purchased their home in Blue Bell, the backyard was a flat expanse of grass bordered by an aging wooden fence. They had two young kids, a dog, and a vision: a backyard where the whole family could spend every summer weekend together — without ever needing to leave the house."

**Beat 2 — The Design Approach:**
How did the Blue Tree team approach the design? What were the key design decisions? What challenges did the property present (grade, drainage, space constraints, soil conditions)?

> _Example copy:_ "Our designer \[Name\] met with the homeowners at the property to walk the space and listen. The yard had a significant grade change from the house to the rear property line — a challenge that most homeowners see as a limitation, but our team saw as an opportunity. The grade allowed for a raised patio with a panoramic view of the pool below, creating a natural separation between the entertaining area and the swimming zone."

**Beat 3 — The Build:**
What did the construction process involve? How did Blue Tree's single-point-of-contact model benefit this specific project? Any noteworthy construction details that demonstrate craftsmanship?

> _Example copy:_ "Because Blue Tree handled every element — pool construction, hardscape installation, landscape planting, lighting, and irrigation — the homeowners had one point of contact throughout the entire build. Our pool construction manager coordinated with the hardscape crew on coping transitions, and our landscape designer specified drought-tolerant native plantings that would thrive in the microclimate created by the pool's southern exposure."

**Design Notes:**

*   Weave 2–3 photos between the story beats (not all photos at the bottom)
*   Pull quotes from the designer or homeowner break up the text
*   Mobile: Single column with full-width photos between text sections

**Word Count Target:** 400–600 words for the story section.
