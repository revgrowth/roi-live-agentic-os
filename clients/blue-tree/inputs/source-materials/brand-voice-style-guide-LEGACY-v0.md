# **Blue Tree Landscaping Brand‑Voice & Style Guide** 
---
## 1. Brand‑Voice Overview (quick‑reference)
| Pillar | How it sounds | Example phrasing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <bold>Authority</bold> | Confident, uses industry‑specific terms, cites certifications. | “Our ICPI‑certified hard‑scape crew installs paver patios that are engineered for lasting durability.” |
| <bold>Professionalism</bold> | Clear, concise, no slang; always finishes sentences with a period. | “We provide a transparent, itemized estimate before work begins.” |
| <bold>Warmth</bold> | First‑person plural, personal gratitude, friendly but never informal. | “We’re thrilled to bring your backyard vision to life—thank you for trusting us.” |
| <bold>Legacy</bold> | References the founding year (1983) and long‑standing community ties. | “For more than 40 years, Blue Tree has shaped the outdoor spaces of historic Philadelphia suburbs.” |
| <bold>Geography Rule</bold> | <bold>Always</bold> describe the service area as <bold>“Southeastern PA”</bold> or list <bold>all counties</bold> (Montgomery, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Philadelphia) in a single, comprehensive phrase. Never use “Montgomery County” alone. | “We proudly serve homeowners throughout <bold>Southeastern PA</bold>—including Montgomery, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Philadelphia counties.” |

## 2. Core Style Rules (punctuation, capitalization, numbers)
| Rule | Correct | Incorrect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <bold>Sentence termination</bold> | Every sentence ends with a period, exclamation point, or question mark. | “Our crews are certified” (missing period) |
| <bold>Serial (Oxford) comma</bold> | Use a comma before the final “and” in a list of three or more items. | “pools, patios and pergolas” → <bold>“pools, patios, and pergolas.”</bold> |
| <bold>Comma after introductory clause</bold> | “When you schedule a free consultation, we’ll begin the design process.” | “When you schedule a free consultation we’ll begin…” |
| <bold>Em‑dash</bold> | Use a single em‑dash (—) with no spaces on either side for emphasis. | “our‑team” or “our – team” |
| <bold>Numbers</bold> | Spell out numbers one‑through‑nine; use numerals for 10+. | “four years” (OK) vs. “4 years” (use numeral) |
| <bold>Degrees & measurements</bold> | Use the degree symbol with a space before the unit (e.g., “30 ° C”). | “30°C” (no space) |
| <bold>Capitalization</bold> | Proper nouns (Southeastern PA, Montgomery County, Blue Tree Landscaping) are capitalized; common nouns are not. | “the southeastern pa region” |
| <bold>Apostrophes</bold> | Use only for possession or contractions; never for plurals. | “the client’s needs” (possession) vs. “the client’s” (incorrect plural) |
| <bold>Hyphenation</bold> | Compound adjectives before a noun are hyphenated. | “full‑service maintenance” (correct) vs. “full service maintenance” (incorrect) |
| <bold>Ellipsis</bold> | Use three periods with a space before and after when indicating a pause. | “…” (no spaces) |
| <bold>Quotation marks</bold> | Double quotes for direct speech; single quotes for a quote within a quote. | “He said, ‘We love your garden.’” |
---
## 3. Geographic Wording (the most‑frequent error)
1. **Primary phrase** – *Southeastern PA*
  - Use this whenever you need a short, catch‑all descriptor.
  - Example: “Blue Tree proudly serves the **Southeastern PA** region.”
2. **Full‑county list** – *Montgomery, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Philadelphia counties*
  - Use when you want to be explicit (e.g., legal disclosures, service‑area maps).
  - Example: “Our service area includes **Montgomery, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Philadelphia counties**.”
3. **Never** write “Montgomery County” **alone** unless it is part of the full list above.
  - Bad: “We work in Montgomery County.”
  - Good: “We work throughout **Southeastern PA**, including Montgomery County.”
4. **Optional “the historic suburbs of Philadelphia”** – can be added after the full‑county list for marketing flair, but keep the county list intact.
  - Example: “Serving **Montgomery, Bucks, Chester, Delaware, and Philadelphia counties**—the historic suburbs of Philadelphia.”

## 5. Proof‑reading Checklist (use before any copy is published)
| ✅ | Item |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Does the copy contain <bold>one</bold> of the two approved geographic phrases? |
| 2 | Are <bold>all</bold> sentences terminated with a period, exclamation point, or question mark? |
| 3 | Are <bold>serial commas</bold> present in every list of three or more items? |
| 4 | Is there a <bold>comma</bold> after every introductory clause? |
| 5 | Are <bold>proper nouns</bold> capitalized correctly (Southeastern PA, Blue Tree Landscaping, etc.)? |
| 6 | Are <bold>numbers</bold> spelled out (1‑9) or shown as numerals (10+) consistently? |
| 7 | Are <bold>hyphenated compound adjectives</bold> used before nouns (full‑service, on‑site, high‑quality)? |
| 8 | Is the <bold>tone</bold> simultaneously authoritative, professional, and warm? |
| 9 | Does the copy reference the <bold>legacy</bold> (1983) where appropriate? |
| 10 | Are <bold>quotes</bold> and <bold>client names</bold> correctly punctuated and attributed? |

## 6. Implementation Tips
1. **Create a master template file** (e.g., `BlueTree_BrandVoice.docx`) that contains all the blocks above. Distribute it to every team member and external agency.
2. **Add a “Style‑Check” column** to any content‑planning spreadsheet; mark “Pass/Fail” after the checklist is run.
3. **Use a grammar‑checking tool** (e.g., Grammarly, ProWritingAid) with a custom rule that flags “Montgomery County” when not accompanied by the full county list.
4. **Schedule quarterly audits** of published assets (website, brochures, ads) to ensure the guide remains in force.
