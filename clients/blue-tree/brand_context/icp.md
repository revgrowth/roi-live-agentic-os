## Primary persona — The Established Southeastern PA Homeowner

**Demographics:** Age 40 to 65. Household income $150K+ (per custom-pool-design-construction.md §1.3). Owns a property in one of the five counties, typically 0.5 to 5+ acres. Often a second or third home in the area, or 10+ years in their current home. Educated and affluent but not an industry professional (v1.1 §11.7 reading-level target: Grade 9 to 11).

**Mindset:**
- Treats the outdoor project as a long-term investment in the property, not a one-time expense.
- Refuses to coordinate three contractors and willing to pay a premium for one accountable party (v1.1 §1.2).
- Values craftsmanship and long-term thinking. Skeptical of high-pressure sales.
- Has likely been burned by a contractor before (a pool company that disappeared, a landscaper who left drainage problems).
- Researches online for three to twelve months before reaching out (v1.1 §9.1).
- Compares two to three contractors before requesting any estimate.

**Project size:** $75K to $400K+ for full backyard transformations including pool, landscape, hardscape. $10K to $80K for landscape-only renovations.

**What they want from Blue Tree:** A single point of contact who manages the project from sketch through years of maintenance. 3D renderings before commitment. Transparent pricing with phased options. The warranty stack published front and center.

**Trigger to inquire:** Kids growing up and the backyard is the last remaining space to invest in. Or kids out of the house and the property is becoming the entertaining hub. Pool dreams fueled by COVID-era research that's now ready to act on.

## Secondary persona — The New Homeowner

**Demographics:** Age 35 to 50. Recently purchased a property in Southeastern PA. Often new construction where the builder did the bare minimum on landscape and outdoor living, or a resale property with an aging or overgrown landscape from the previous owner.

**Mindset:**
- Wants the property to feel intentional and finished.
- Cares about curb appeal and seasonal interest.
- Open to designer-led recommendations because they don't have strong opinions on plant varieties yet.
- May be working from a Pinterest board or photos from elsewhere they've lived.
- Patient with the establishment period if the designer sets expectations.

**Project size:** $10K to $80K landscape-led. May add hardscape or a pool in a later phase ($150K to $400K combined over two to four years).

**What they want from Blue Tree:** A designer who listens to vision, walks the property, and produces a 3D rendering. Local sourcing emphasis (90% from local growers). USDA Zone 6b and 7a expertise. Phased plan so year one is curb appeal, year two is the backyard, year three adds the pool or hardscape.

## Service-tier segmentation

Per v1.1 §9.1-§9.5, Blue Tree's pages should be designed with a primary segment in mind even when they serve all.

### Pool buyers (Segment 1, "The Pool-First Buyer")

- Profile: Affluent homeowner, age 35 to 60, considering $75K to $200K+ pool investment. Often first major outdoor project. Researching three to twelve months.
- Mindset: Excited about the dream, anxious about budget, worried about being taken advantage of. Researches warranty, equipment, and contractor reputation in depth.
- Voice cues for them: Direct on price ranges. Pool construction managers (Mike Wadsworth, John Kostesich) prominent. Justin Acal and Christopher DiVito as design leads. Show 3D rendering proof. Address the integration. (v1.1 §9.1)

### Full backyard transformation buyers

- Profile: Often the same Segment 1 buyer once they realize the pool isn't the whole project. Total spend $150K to $400K+ including landscape and hardscape integration. [derived inference combining v1.1 §6.2 row 5 "Repeat customer" with §9.6 segment cross-application]
- Mindset: Wants the pool, the deck, the landscape, the drainage, the lighting, and the restoration as one project. Refuses to coordinate three contractors.
- Voice cues: Lead with the Blue Tree Complete Backyard Process (v1.1 §7.1). Show comparison chart (Blue Tree vs. typical pool-only builder).

### Hardscape-led buyers (Segment 3, "The Hardscape Builder")

- Profile: Homeowner planning a patio, outdoor kitchen, fire feature, or retaining wall. Often the natural next phase after a pool or as part of a larger backyard plan.
- Project size: $15K to $100K+.
- Mindset: Cares about material durability, slip resistance, heat retention near pools, longevity. Detail-oriented.
- Voice cues: ICPI certification (Chad, James Gonczkowski, Justin Ryen). Two-year workmanship warranty. Specific material brands (A&B Natural Stone). Technical detail on bases, drainage, joint stabilization. (v1.1 §9.3)

### Healthy Yards (turf care) buyers (Segment 4, "The Lawn Care Customer")

- Profile: Homeowner with an underperforming lawn. Often calls during spring rush or summer dry spell when the lawn looks worst.
- Project size: $400 to $2,000+ annually depending on program tier.
- Mindset: Frustrated with previous lawn care company or DIY results. Wants quick fix but is teachable on timelines. Price-sensitive but values expertise.
- Voice cues: Mark Peasley's plainspoken teaching voice. "A lawn is a representation of how it's been treated the last three years." Tiered program clarity. Honest about timelines and the homeowner's role. (v1.1 §9.4)

### Premier Outdoor Services (maintenance) buyers (Segment 5, "The Long-Term Property Partner")

- Profile: Existing Blue Tree customer or homeowner who values one-company accountability for ongoing maintenance.
- Project size: Recurring annual maintenance $2K to $15K+ plus occasional improvement projects.
- Mindset: Treats Blue Tree as the property's general contractor for outdoor work. Calls Blue Tree first for anything outside the house.
- Voice cues: Relationship-first. Long-tenured employee names (Aggie Kriebel since 2008, Jose Zavala since 2011, Maureen Mattiola since 2016). References to 13 to 14 year average tenure. Stories of multi-decade client relationships (Jeff's example of clients of 25 to 30 years whose kids are now hiring Blue Tree). (v1.1 §9.5)

## Disqualifiers (who's NOT a fit)

Per v1.1 §10.1 nightmare client language, §7.5 scope honesty, and §9 segment guidance:

- Customers who only care about the lowest price. Blue Tree is rarely the cheapest quote. Jérôme's framing: "We provide lasting value rather than the lowest price."
- Customers who treat contractors disrespectfully.
- Customers who demand mid-project changes that should have been resolved at design (forcing the project off-scope after the fact).
- Customers wanting above-ground pools, vinyl liner pools, or fiberglass pools.
- Customers wanting a full lawn sprinkler system. Blue Tree refers to specialists.
- Customers wanting tree care beyond stump grinding and minor removal. Blue Tree refers to partner tree specialists.
- Customers wanting pool emergency repair as a standalone service.
- Commercial landscaping clients at launch. Residential focus. Commercial may return later.

## Trigger events (when ICPs convert)

Inferred from custom-pool-design-construction.md, our-story-brand.md, and v1.1 §9. Trigger events are the moments that turn a researcher into an inquiry.

- **Kids growing up:** Family realizes the backyard is the last remaining space to invest in. Pool dreams crystallize when the youngest is old enough to swim.
- **Kids leaving home:** Property becomes the entertaining hub. Renovation budget shifts from interior to exterior.
- **New construction completion:** Builder finished the house. Front yard is sod and three foundation shrubs. Homeowner wants to make the property livable.
- **Resale purchase:** New owner inherited overgrown or aging landscape from a previous owner. Wants the property to feel intentional again.
- **Previous-contractor disappointment:** A pool company disappeared mid-project. A landscaper left drainage problems. A patio cracked in year two with no warranty support. Homeowner is now shopping for one company that won't repeat the experience.
- **Multi-year phase trigger:** Existing Blue Tree client ready for Phase 2 (the patio after the pool, the lighting after the landscape).
- **Lifestyle change:** Working from home permanently. Property becomes the daily environment, not the weekend retreat.
- **COVID-era pool research finally acting:** [derived inference] Customers who started researching pools in 2020-2021 and now have the budget and contractor confidence to act in 2026.
- **Spring or summer lawn frustration:** Healthy Yards inquiries spike when the lawn looks worst (Mark Peasley audio, v1.1 §9.4).
