## 2. Founder Voice Profile, Jeff Mattiola

Jeff is the primary expert author across the site. His voice is the default voice when no other team member is named.

### 2.1 Background (Verified)

- **Title:** Owner, President
- **Tenure:** Has been a key figure in the landscaping industry since 1983
- **Joined Blue Tree:** 1989 as partner; sole owner from 1992
- **Education:** University of Pittsburgh graduate
- **Specialties:** Landscape, hardscape, and pool installations; company sales
- **Lives in:** Upper Providence with his wife, Maureen Mattiola (Blue Tree's Marketing Manager since 2016), and their four children (Andrew, David, John, Meg)
- **Outside of work:** Reading on the beach, fishing, biking on local trails, vacationing with family, relaxing by the pool

### 2.2 Communication DNA

Jeff speaks the way a contractor who has run a 70-person operation for three decades speaks. Confident. Direct. Plainspoken. He uses concrete operational detail without marketing veneer. He references specific moments (the 2008 downturn, COVID, the year they only built four pools) rather than abstract claims. He admits mistakes and explains pivots. He refuses corporate language.

### 2.3 Natural Speech Patterns

From the audio recordings, Jeff's prose patterns:

- **Short declarative sentences.** "We build what we design." "I had no clue what I was doing." "It's a survival mental moment, so we can't adapt."
- **Numbered or sequenced lists in conversation.** When walking through history, he counts years and events out loud.
- **First-person plural ("we," "our") dominates.** First-person singular ("I") appears in origin-story moments and when describing his personal philosophy.
- **Concrete nouns and verbs.** "Grit, determination, and the sheer desire of success." "Excavation, rebar, plumbing, gunite, plaster, tile, coping, decking, the whole nine yards." Specific over abstract.
- **Plain-English finance and operations vocabulary.** "Capital intensive." "Self-funded." "We were dictated by the builder." "Variance." "Impervious surface." Industry terms appear without strain.
- **He answers the unspoken question.** Jeff anticipates the doubt and addresses it before the customer voices it. ("You're going to have unknowns when you're digging a pool. As soon as we know, we reach out.")

### 2.4 Signature Phrases (Use Verbatim or Paraphrase Tightly)

- "We build what we design."
- "We need to do it right."
- "It's not just about the project, it's about the relationship."
- "We weren't going to make money for the first couple years. It was going to cost us money, but we were going to build them right."
- "All you have in life is your reputation. Don't screw it up." (From Jeff's father; Jeff cites this as his guiding principle.)
- "We're a corporation, but we're not a corporation."
- "Our designs are custom, they're realistic, they're executable."
- "Complex projects are not a problem. We solve it through collaboration, not shortcuts."
- "We don't want any bad blood between us and the customers."

### 2.5 Origin Story Voice (For About, Our Story, Leadership Bios)

When telling the origin story, Jeff's voice does three things:

1. **Credits family support before crediting himself.** "I was able to stay at home and not having to live out on my own, so I was able to save money."
2. **Names the hard parts plainly.** "Blood, sweat, and tears went into building this." "They told me they were not lending, or we can't do this, or your ratios don't look right. I figured out a way to do it."
3. **Frames the pivots as adaptation, not strategy.** The 2008 downturn led to four-day work weeks. COVID brought an essential designation and kept the crew working. Failed ventures (the tow truck, Christmas trees, driveway sealcoating, the landscape depot) became "we tried, we learned, we refocused."

In copy, follow this pattern. Lead with what was tried. Name what worked and what didn't. End with the lesson that shaped how Blue Tree operates today.

### 2.6 Jeff's Three Operating Principles

> Note (v1.2, 2026-05-12): The "element of surprise is minimal" phrasing is not transcribable to a specific recording and is not used as a verbatim Jeff quote. The pricing-transparency principle remains; the specific phrasing was removed from §2.4 signature phrases.

1. **Do it right or don't do it.** When entering pool construction, Jeff hired experienced builders rather than learning by trial and error. "We can't focus the time and energy learning the process from scratch. If we're going to do this, we need to do it right."
2. **Be transparent on price.** "It's very important to me to be upfront as possible with pricing, so that the element of surprise is minimal."
3. **Long-term relationships over short-term margins.** Jeff cites clients of 25 to 30 years. Their kids now hire Blue Tree. He has changed a longtime client's lightbulb because she needed help.

These three principles anchor any About, Our Story, Why Choose Us, or Process page.

### 2.7 What Jeff Won't Say

- Hype language ("game-changer," "revolutionary," "industry-leading")
- Corporate hedging ("we strive to," "we endeavor to," "best-in-class")
- Anything that sounds like a pitch deck
- Anything that promises a single outcome on a project where the homeowner's behavior affects the result (watering, mowing, maintenance)
- Anything that compares Blue Tree to a specific named competitor

## 4. Brand Personality & Values

### 4.1 Personality Traits

Blue Tree is:

- **Authoritative without being corporate.** Speaks from 43 years of field experience, not from a boardroom. References specific operational details (soil conditions, township permitting, equipment choices) that a generalist could not fabricate.
- **Direct and transparent on money.** Addresses cost openly. Names price ranges. Explains what drives pricing variation. Refuses the "call for pricing" dodge.
- **Warm and relationship-oriented.** Frames every project as the beginning of a long-term relationship, not a transaction.
- **Low-pressure and collaborative.** Designers lead with listening, not closing. Free consultations with no obligation.
- **Legacy-proud without being nostalgic.** References 1983 and 43-year track record as proof of staying power, not as sentimentality.
- **Self-aware about scope.** Says "we don't do that" when asked about services Blue Tree doesn't offer (sprinkler systems, emergency pool repair, tree care beyond stump grinding, commercial work). Recommends specialists.

### 4.2 Core Values

| Value | What It Means in Copy |
|---|---|
| **Reputation above all** | Jeff's father's rule. Drives the refusal to cut corners or take a job we can't do right. |
| **Do it right or don't do it** | The reason Blue Tree only built four pools in year one of the pool division. The reason it now sub-contracts tree work above a certain height. |
| **Long-term relationships** | The way every audio recording closes. Customers become friends. Kids of original customers hire us. |
| **Transparency on money and timeline** | "We price transparently. The bid is the price." Upfront pricing, honest about hidden conditions, clear about why timeline is months not weeks. ### 11.3 Punctuation Rules (Critical)

**Em dashes are banned in all Blue Tree copy.** The brand writes without them.

Reasons:

1. Em dashes have become an unmistakable AI tell. Pattern-matching readers and detectors flag them instantly.
2. The brand voice favors short declarative sentences over interrupted ones.
3. Where an em dash would appear, the writer should use one of the following replacements depending on intent:

| Em-Dash Function | Replacement |
|---|---|
| Parenthetical clause | Comma pair, or parentheses, or rewrite as two sentences |
| Sudden break for emphasis | Period and new sentence |
| Range (5 to 10) | "to" (already preferred) |
| Definition or appositive | Comma, or "is" / "are" / "namely" |
| List introduction | Colon |

**Em-dash compliance is part of the pre-delivery checklist (§18). Any document containing an em dash is returned for revision.**

### 11.4 Sentence-Level Rules

- **No adverbs.** Strip -ly modifiers (really, very, simply, actually, just, quite, perhaps, certainly). They weaken prose. Exceptions: "only" when essential, "early" or "late" when referring to time of season.
- **No softeners.** Cut "just," "simply," "actually," "really," "very," "quite," "perhaps."
- **No throat-clearing openers.** "So," "Well," "Now," "Look." Cut.
- **No meta-commentary.** "Let me explain," "Here's why that matters," "It's important to note." Cut.
- **No passive voice.** "The proposal will be delivered" becomes "We deliver the proposal."
- **No narrator-from-a-distance voice.** "One might argue" becomes "Some homeowners ask."
- **Vary sentence length.** No three consecutive sentences the same length. Break the third.
- **No em dashes.** See §11.3.
- **No dramatic one-word fragments.** "Quality. Design. Trust." Never.
- **No binary contrasts.** "It's not X. It's Y." Never.
- **No rhetorical questions answered in the next sentence.** "What separates Blue Tree from other pool builders? Everything." Never.
- **No three-item lists.** Use two items, or use four-plus genuine items.
- **No pull-quote-ready sentences in body copy.** If a sentence reads like it belongs in a poster, rewrite it.

### 11.5 Other Punctuation & Mechanics

| Rule | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence terminations | Every sentence ends with `.` `!` or `?` | Missing punctuation |
| Oxford comma | "pools, patios, and pergolas" | "pools, patios and pergolas" |
| Comma after intro clause | "When you schedule a free consultation, we'll begin." | Missing comma |
| Em dash | Banned. Replace per §11.3. | Any em-dash character |
| En dash (ranges) | "five to ten years" or "5-year" (hyphen for compound) | "5–10" in body copy |
| Numbers 1 to 9 | Spelled out ("four years," "seven counties") | "4 years," "7 counties" |
| Numbers 10+ | Numerals ("43 years," "70 employees") | "forty-three years" |
| Hyphenated compound adjectives | "full-service," "design-build," "on-site," "two-year warranty" | "full service," "design build" |
| Quotation marks | Double quotes for direct speech; single for nested | "He said, 'we'll be there.'" |
| Apostrophes | Possession and contractions only | Never for plurals |
| Ellipsis | Three periods with spaces: ` . . . ` | "..." (no spaces) |

## 12. Content Structure Patterns

Each page type follows a defined structural pattern. Writers and editors should reference these patterns before drafting.

### 12.1 Pillar Page (Pool, Landscape, Hardscape, Healthy Yards, Premier Outdoor Services)

Word count: 1,800 to 2,500 words.

Conversion spine:

1. **Hero.** H1, subheadline with answer capsule, primary CTA, secondary CTA (form-first; phone in footer only).
2. **Trust Logo Strip.** Industry affiliations (NALP, PLNA, ICPI, APSP, CBP), certifications, supplier partners.
3. **Problem Framing.** What the reader is trying to solve. Acknowledges the pain.
4. **Solution Overview.** Named approach (The Blue Tree Complete Backyard Process).
5. **Proof Section #1.** Testimonial, video, or case study excerpt.
6. **Named Process.** Step-by-step walk-through (7-step Complete Backyard Process or service-specific variant).
7. **Mid-CTA.** Inline form or anchored button.
8. **Proof Section #2.** Trust bar with numbers (43 years, 90+ employees, lifetime warranty, etc.) and second testimonial.
9. **Pricing.** Range with framing for what drives variation. No "call for pricing."
10. **Service Areas.** Southeastern PA + full county list. Internal link to Service Areas Hub.
11. **FAQ.** 8 to 12 questions with answer capsules. FAQPage schema applied.
12. **Guarantee / Warranty.** Specific terms published front and center.
13. **Final CTA.** Strong close. Form-first.

Schema: Service + LocalBusiness + FAQPage. Author attribution to Jeff or service-specific expert.

### 12.2 Cluster / Supporting Service Page

Word count: 1,200 to 1,800 words.

Conversion spine: same as pillar but condensed. Skip the trust logo strip. Replace generic proof with service-specific proof. FAQ shorter (5 to 8 questions).

Schema: Service + Article (or HowTo where applicable) + FAQPage. Author attribution to the specific expert.

### 12.3 Location Page, County

Word count: 1,500 to 2,000 words.

Sections:

1. Hero with county name and county-specific subheadline.
2. Why Southeastern PA homeowners in (County) choose Blue Tree.
3. Towns served within the county (link to town pages where they exist).
4. Local entity co-occurrence (suppliers, township references, area-specific plant or material notes).
5. Project examples from the county.
6. Service offerings summary.
7. FAQs specific to the county (impervious surface rules, common drainage issues, etc.).
8. Final CTA.

Schema: LocalBusiness with area served + FAQPage + relevant Service refs.

### 12.4 Location Page, Town or Service+Town Hybrid

Word count: 1,200 to 1,600 words for town pages. Pool Builders in (Town) hybrids are 1,800 to 2,400 words.

Sections:

1. Hero with town and county.
2. Why we work in (Town).
3. Past projects in or near the town.
4. Town-specific notes (architectural style, lot size, soil, local landmarks).
5. Service offerings.
6. FAQs.
7. Final CTA.

**Skippack page special rules:** Founded in Norristown 1983, in Skippack since 2008. Do NOT use "Founded in 1983 right here in this community" framing.

### 12.5 Blog Post / Resource Page

Word count: 1,500 to 2,500 for evergreen; 800 to 1,200 for timely.

Structure:

1. Hero image and H1.
2. Author byline with headshot, name, role, short bio block.
3. **TL;DR / Answer Capsule.** 50 to 100 words. The directly extractable answer to the post's primary query.
4. **Body.** H2 sections with H3 sub-sections. Real data, named team members, specific examples.
5. **FAQ.** 3 to 6 related questions.
6. **Internal Links.** Related blog posts, related service pages.
7. **Final CTA.** Soft. Link to relevant service or contact.

Schema: Article (or BlogPosting) + Person (author) + FAQPage + Organization. Speakable schema on TL;DR.

**Comments disabled.** Social share buttons present.

## 14. Ghostwriting Rules

When writing as Jeff Mattiola or another named team member, follow these rules to keep the voice consistent and the attribution credible.

### 14.1 Writing as Jeff Mattiola

**Use Jeff's voice when:**

- The page is About Us, Our Story, Why Choose Blue Tree, the home page, or any page with no clearer subject-matter expert.
- The content discusses the company's history, philosophy, scale, ownership, or full-property model.
- The content is a foundational statement of what Blue Tree does.

**Voice rules for Jeff:**

1. **Short sentences mixed with longer ones.** Vary length. Jeff doesn't speak in paragraph-long sentences.
2. **First-person plural ("we") for company actions; first-person singular ("I") only for personal-history statements.** "We've been here since 1983" but "I bought out the original partner three years after joining."
3. **Concrete operational detail.** Name materials, years, names of partners or employees, specific incidents (2008, COVID, the four-pool first year). Avoid abstract claims.
4. **Acknowledge mistakes.** "I had no clue what I was doing." "We tried it. It didn't work. We shut it down." Mistakes humanize the brand.
5. **End sections with the principle, not the pitch.** "All you have in life is your reputation. Don't screw it up." Not "Call us today to start your project."
6. **Never use Jeff to deliver a hard sell.** Jeff is the elder statesman voice. CTAs in Jeff's voice are framed as invitations, not closes.

**Words Jeff would never say (do not put in his mouth):**

- "Leverage," "synergy," "best-in-class," "industry-leading"
- "Game-changer," "move the needle"
- "I'm passionate about" or "I'm excited to"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Solutions" as a corporate filler
- "Seamless" or "seamlessly"
- "Elevate" (used to mean improve)
- Any modifier ending in -ly except "early" or "late"

### 14.2 Writing as Chad Ochnich

**Use Chad's voice when:**

- The page discusses hardscape, turf care, or the operational backbone of Blue Tree.
- The content is project-centric, telling specific stories of past customers and projects.

1. **Anchor every point to a specific project.** Sydney Cox in Blue Bell. The McGarvey family in Royersford. The Lower Granite impervious-surface variance.

3. **Calm, grounded, no drama.** Even on the toughest projects, the voice is matter-of-fact.

### 14.3 Writing as Jérôme Besnard

**Use Jérôme's voice when:**

1. **Three categories at a time.** Jérôme groups objections into three (money, timing, trust). Use that structure in copy.

3. **Frame value vs. price.** "We provide lasting value rather than the lowest price."
4. **Educate without condescension.** Jérôme assumes the reader is intelligent but unfamiliar.

### 14.4 Writing as Mark Peasley

**Use Mark's voice when:**

- The page is Healthy Yards pillar or any turf-care cluster page.
- The content explains the slow process of lawn improvement.
- The content addresses mosquito and tick control.

**Voice rules for Mark:**

2. **Honest about limits.** "Some weeds are more difficult to remove than others." "There's not much I can do as far as quick fixes go in lawns."
3. **Teach the homeowner's role.** Watering, mowing, blade sharpness, soil all matter alongside the program.
4. **Treat the relationship as ongoing.** "After two or three years, your relationship is very steady and strong."
