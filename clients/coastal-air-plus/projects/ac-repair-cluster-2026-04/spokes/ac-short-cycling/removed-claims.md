**Intent:** transactional (CPC $7.20, KD 10 — highest difficulty in batch 2)
**Mode:** audit-and-fix replacement of legacy stub (1,455 words, 1 H2, no real body)

This spoke is transactional intent, so the CTA emphasis is heavier and the "stakes" framing (compressor wear, electric bill impact) is more pronounced. The no-fabrication rule made the stakes section harder to write — the natural urgency narrative wants specific numbers (compressor lifespan, kilowatt-hour impact, dollar costs) that aren't sourceable in-session.

## What stayed (mechanism descriptions, not stat claims)

- "Compressor wears with every start" — basic mechanical wear mechanism.
- "Capacitor stores the run charge for the compressor" — component description.
- "Contactor closes the circuit to the compressor under load" — component description.
- "Oversized unit hits temperature setpoint before completing dehumidification" — psychrometric mechanism.
- "Outdoor coil dumps heat from refrigerant cycle to outside air" — refrigeration cycle description.
- "Low refrigerant drops the coil's operating pressure and temperature" — thermodynamic mechanism.

The transactional intent of this spoke made the stakes section the hardest to write without quantification. The natural sales pitch would lead with "you'll save $X on energy bills" or "extend your compressor's life by Y years." Neither is sourceable in-session, so both were dropped in favor of qualitative framing ("meaningfully less efficient," "trades a routine repair today for a compressor replacement sooner"). The CTA strength comes from the urgency framing (every cycle adds wear) rather than from quantified loss claims.
