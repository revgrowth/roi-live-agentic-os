CHARLESTON COUNTY CLIMATE

## Common AC Problems Charleston Homeowners Face

Charleston sits in one of the most demanding climates for residential cooling systems in the United States. Average summer temperatures regularly exceed 95°F, and relative humidity holds at 74% or higher from June through September. Salt air infiltration accelerates corrosion on copper refrigerant lines — particularly on properties east of the Ashley River, on James Island, and within two miles of the harbor. Here are the problems we see most often in Charleston County homes.

Short Cycling · Oversized System

### Humidity Overload

Lowcountry humidity makes your AC work double duty—cooling and dehumidifying. When the system can't keep up, you get a home that feels clammy even at 72°F. Oversized AC units are a common culprit in newer Charleston subdivisions like West Ashley, where builder-grade systems may short-cycle rather than properly dehumidify.

Refrigerant Leak · R-410A · R-22

### Refrigerant Leaks

Low refrigerant levels are the number-one cause of warm air blowing from your vents. In the Lowcountry, salt air and coastal moisture accelerate corrosion on copper refrigerant lines, making leaks more common here than in inland areas. We test and recharge with R-410A or identify if your older system still runs on phased-out R-22 Freon.

Compressor Failure · Capacitor

### Compressor Failure

The compressor is the heart of your air conditioning system. Extended run times during Charleston's brutal summers put enormous strain on this component. Symptoms include warm air from vents, the outdoor unit humming but not starting, or the system tripping your circuit breaker repeatedly.

Condensate Drain Clog · Evaporator Coil

### Clogged Condensate Drains

High humidity means your AC produces more condensation than systems in drier climates. When the condensate drain line clogs with algae, mold, or debris, water backs up into your home. In Charleston, we see this especially in older homes in the Historic District and Goose Creek where drainage systems may not have been updated.

West Ashley · James Island · New Construction

### First-Wave Repairs in Charleston's Newer Communities

Homes in West Ashley, James Island, Johns Island, and Daniel Island built between 2015 and 2024 are now hitting their first major repair windows. Capacitors typically fail at the 7–10 year mark. Refrigerant lines in homes approaching a decade old show early corrosion in Lowcountry conditions — accelerated by salt air exposure this close to the harbor and coast. Builder-grade systems in newer Charleston subdivisions were often sized for cost efficiency rather than humidity performance — meaning short-cycling and inadequate dehumidification are the most common complaints we see from these neighborhoods. If your home is in one of these areas and your system is 7 years or older, a preventive diagnostic is worth scheduling before peak summer.

Catching these problems early is the difference between a $200 repair and a $2,000 compressor replacement. Read our complete guide to [signs your AC needs professional repair](https://coastalcarolinahvac.com/signs-ac-needs-repair/) to know exactly when to call.

## Why Charleston AC Systems Fail Faster Than Inland Systems

Charleston averages more than 45 days per year with a heat index above 100°F — among the highest of any metro area on the East Coast. From June through September, relative humidity holds at 74% or higher. That combination forces residential AC systems to run longer cycles, remove more moisture per hour, and operate under thermal loads that exceed what most systems were sized to handle.

The result is accelerated wear on three components in particular: compressor windings overheat faster under sustained high-load operation, condensate drain lines clog more frequently because the system produces 30–50% more moisture per cycle than systems in drier climates, and copper refrigerant lines corrode faster due to salt air infiltration — particularly on properties east of the Ashley River, on James Island, and in any neighborhood within two miles of the harbor.

These aren't generic HVAC problems. They're Charleston problems. A technician who services systems in Columbia or Greenville doesn't encounter these failure patterns at the same frequency. Our technicians do, because Charleston is the only market we serve.

EARLY WARNING SIGNS

## Signs Your AC Needs Professional Repair

Not every AC issue is an emergency, but every symptom is a signal. Catching problems early prevents small repairs from becoming major replacements. Here are the warning signs Charleston homeowners should watch for, especially during our demanding cooling season.

Warm or inconsistent air from vents+

If your system runs but the air coming out isn't cold, you may have a refrigerant leak, a failing compressor, or restricted airflow from a dirty evaporator coil.

Unusual noises+

Grinding often indicates a failing fan motor bearing. Buzzing can signal electrical issues. Clicking at startup may point to a bad capacitor or contactor. None of these resolve on their own.

Higher-than-normal energy bills+

A sudden spike in your Dominion Energy bill often means your system is working overtime to compensate for a failing component.

Short cycling+

If your AC turns on and off every few minutes rather than running steady cycles, the system may be oversized, low on refrigerant, or have a malfunctioning thermostat.

Moisture or ice on the indoor unit+

Ice on your evaporator coil or water pooling around the air handler signals restricted airflow or low refrigerant. In the Lowcountry's humidity, a frozen coil can quickly lead to water damage.

Musty or burning smells+

A musty smell from your vents can indicate mold growth inside the ductwork or on the evaporator coil—a real concern in humid Charleston. Burning smells suggest electrical issues that need immediate attention.

For a deeper look at each of these symptoms and when they cross the line from 'keep an eye on it' to 'call a pro now,' read our complete guide to [signs your AC needs professional repair](https://coastalcarolinahvac.com/signs-ac-needs-repair/).

## AC Repair vs. AC Replacement: How to Know

Not every AC problem requires a full system replacement. But sometimes repair costs approach or exceed the value of putting money into an aging system. Knowing the difference saves you money and frustration.

### ✓ CONSIDER REPAIR WHEN

- Your system is under 10 years old
- The repair costs less than 50% of a new unit
- The problem is isolated to a single component (capacitor, contactor, thermostat)
- The system uses current R-410A refrigerant

### × CONSIDER REPLACEMENT WHEN

- Your AC is 15+ years old
- You're facing a major component failure (compressor, evaporator coil) on an aging system
- The unit still uses R-22 Freon (phased out and increasingly expensive)
- You've needed multiple repairs in the past two years

Our technicians will always give you an honest assessment. If repair makes sense, we'll fix it. If replacement makes more financial sense, we'll explain why. ### If Your System Uses R-410A Refrigerant: What Charleston Homeowners Need to Know in 2025–2026

The EPA's production and import ban on R-410A refrigerant took effect January 1, 2025. R-410A was the dominant residential refrigerant for roughly 15 years and is present in the majority of Charleston-area homes with systems installed between 2010 and 2024.

What this means practically: if your R-410A system develops a refrigerant leak, the repair cost is higher than it was two years ago because supply is limited and prices are rising. A leak repair that cost $350 in 2022 may run $500–$700 today depending on the charge required. The economics of repairing a refrigerant leak on an older R-410A system now need to be weighed against replacement with a newer R-454B or R-32 system.

We'll tell you which refrigerant your system uses during the diagnostic, give you the current recharge cost, and walk you through whether repair or replacement makes more sense given your system's age and condition. No pressure — just the math.

## Common Charleston AC Repair Questions

How much does AC repair cost in Charleston, SC?+

Most AC repairs in Charleston cost between $150 and $650. Capacitor replacement runs $150–$300. Refrigerant recharge (including leak detection) runs $250–$600 depending on the refrigerant type and charge required — R-410A costs have risen significantly since the January 2025 production ban. Compressor repair or replacement can reach $1,200–$2,500. We provide upfront flat-rate pricing after diagnosis, before any work begins.

Why is my AC running but not cooling my Charleston home?+

The most common causes are low refrigerant from a leak, a dirty evaporator or condenser coil, a failing compressor, or ductwork leaks dumping cooled air into your attic. In Charleston's humidity, a clogged air filter alone can freeze the evaporator coil and shut down cooling within hours. Check your filter first. If it's clean and the system still isn't cooling, call for a diagnostic.

Do you repair AC systems in Charleston's historic homes?+

Yes. We regularly service homes in the Historic District, Harleston Village, Wagener Terrace, and other pre-1950s neighborhoods where ductwork access, pier-and-beam foundations, and original construction create repair challenges that standard residential HVAC companies aren't prepared for. We ask the right questions before arriving so we come equipped.

My AC system uses R-410A. Is it still worth repairing?+

It depends on the repair and the system's age. Minor fixes — capacitor, contactor, thermostat — are still cost-effective on R-410A systems regardless of age. Refrigerant leak repairs are more expensive now due to the 2025 production ban, so the math changes on older systems. Compressor replacement on an R-410A system over 12 years old rarely makes financial sense. We'll give you the specific numbers during the diagnostic.

How quickly can you get to my home in Charleston for AC repair?+

Most Charleston-area service calls are answered within 2–4 hours. We dispatch from Summerville and have technicians covering West Ashley, James Island, Mount Pleasant, Daniel Island, North Charleston, and the peninsula. Same-day service is available for calls placed before 2 PM.

What AC brands do you repair in Charleston?+

We service all major residential brands including Trane, Carrier, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, Daikin, York, and Amana. As a Daikin partner, we stock Daikin parts on every truck. We also repair ductless mini-split systems from Mitsubishi, Fujitsu, and LG — which are common in Charleston's older homes that lack existing ductwork.

How often should I have my AC serviced in Charleston?+

Twice per year — once in early spring before the cooling season starts and once in fall. Charleston's cooling season runs March through October, which is longer than most of the country. High humidity accelerates coil fouling, condensate drain clogging, and capacitor wear faster than in drier climates. Regular maintenance catches these before they become emergency calls during peak summer heat.

Does Charleston's salt air affect my AC differently than inland systems?+

Yes — significantly. Properties east of the Ashley River, on James Island, and within two miles of the harbor experience accelerated corrosion on copper refrigerant lines from salt air infiltration. This makes refrigerant leaks more common in coastal Charleston than in inland markets like Columbia or Greenville. Outdoor condenser coils also accumulate salt deposits that reduce heat transfer efficiency over time. Systems in these zones benefit from annual coil cleaning and more frequent refrigerant pressure checks than the manufacturer's standard maintenance schedule recommends.

What causes short cycling — my AC keeps turning on and off?+

Short cycling is usually caused by an oversized system, a failing capacitor, low refrigerant triggering safety shutoffs, or a thermostat malfunction. In newer Charleston developments, builder-grade systems are often oversized for the actual cooling load, which causes short cycles and inadequate dehumidification — the home reaches the set temperature but still feels clammy because the system never runs long enough to properly remove moisture. Read our full guide on [AC short cycling causes and fixes](https://coastalcarolinahvac.com/ac-short-cycling/).

Is it worth repairing a 15-year-old AC system in Charleston?+

For minor repairs — capacitor, contactor, thermostat — yes, even on a 15-year-old system. For major component failures like compressor replacement or evaporator coil leaks, replacement usually makes more financial sense, especially given Charleston's 8-month cooling season that puts significantly more annual hours on equipment than the national average. If the system also uses phased-out R-22 Freon, replacement is almost always the right call. We will give you the honest comparison during the diagnostic.
