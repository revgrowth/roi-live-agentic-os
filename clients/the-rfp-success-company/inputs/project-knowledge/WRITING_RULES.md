# Writing Rules — Stop Slop

These rules apply to ALL prose produced for this project (and as Jason's standing preference across all work). Apply them when writing, editing, or revising any deliverable.

---

## Banned Phrases (Never Use)

- "Here's the thing"
- "Let that sink in"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It's worth noting"
- "Let me be clear"
- "At the end of the day"
- "And that's okay"
- "It's not this, but that"
- "Navigate the complex landscape"
- "Lean into"
- "Unpack"
- "Double down"
- "Deep dive"
- "Game-changer"
- "Move the needle"
- "At its core"
- "In today's world"
- "When it comes to"
- "It turns out that"
- "The reality is"
- "What makes X unique"
- "This is where it gets interesting"

## Banned Structures

- **Binary contrasts** — "It's not X. It's Y."
- **Dramatic one-word fragments** — "Purpose. Clarity. Impact."
- **Negative listings** — "Not because X. Because Y."
- **Rhetorical questions** immediately answered
- **Three-item lists** — use two instead
- **Em-dash reveals**
- **Pull-quote-ready sentences**

## Kill

- All adverbs (-ly words)
- All softeners — just, simply, actually, really, very, quite, perhaps, certainly
- All throat-clearing openers
- All meta-commentary — "Let me explain," "Here's why that matters"
- Passive voice
- Narrator-from-a-distance voice — "One might argue"

## Always

- Vary sentence length
- State facts directly
- Trust the reader
- End paragraphs differently each time
- If it sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it

## Self-Check Before Delivering

- Three consecutive sentences same length → break one
- Paragraph ends with punchy one-liner → vary it
- Em-dash before a reveal → remove it
- Explaining a metaphor → trust it to land

## RFP Success Brand Voice Layer

In addition to the general anti-slop rules above, these brand-specific rules apply to all RFP Success content:

### Words TO USE

| Category | Words |
|---|---|
| Winning | Win, succeed, capture, secure, award |
| Strategy | Strategic, intentional, deliberate, positioned, aligned |
| Partnership | Partner, collaborate, team, alongside, together |
| Expertise | Expert, seasoned, proven, specialized, authority |
| Results | Results, outcomes, impact, ROI, performance, revenue |
| Emotion | Confident, empowered, relieved, proud, excited |
| Wine-specific | Practical, efficient, save time, reduce chaos, smart, scrappy |
| Champagne-specific | Premium, strategic, investment, partnership, competitive advantage, intentional |

### Words TO AVOID (Brand-Specific)

| Category | Words |
|---|---|
| Fear-based | Fail, lose, risk (as scare tactic), danger, threat |
| Vague | Maybe, possibly, might, could potentially |
| Corporate jargon | Synergize, leverage, paradigm, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge |
| Guarantees | Guarantee, promise, ensure (re: winning) |
| Desperation | Affordable, cheap, discount, deal, bargain |

### Brand-Specific DO's

1. Lead with results and specific numbers
2. Use real client stories and quotes
3. Celebrate client wins genuinely
4. Be direct — say what you mean
5. Show empathy for the RFP struggle
6. Use Lisa's natural analogies and metaphors
7. End every piece with an actionable next step
8. Speak TO the client about their needs, not ABOUT yourselves
9. Back every claim with evidence
10. Make it fun — RFPs don't have to be painful

### Brand-Specific DON'Ts

1. Use fear-based tactics or scare language
2. Promise or guarantee wins
3. Be passive or wishy-washy
4. Talk about yourself more than the client
5. Offer discounts or signal desperation
6. Over-complicate — keep it simple and readable
7. Use AI-generated content without heavy human editing
8. Ignore the evaluator's perspective
9. Forget the CTA

## AI Signal Elimination Framework

Documents must read as human-expert-authored. AI signal shows up at four levels:

### Word-Level
- No "delve," "navigate," "unlock," "harness," "leverage," "robust," "seamless," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class"
- No empty modifiers — "truly," "really," "very," "incredibly"
- Use specific nouns over abstract ones — "the evaluator's scoring sheet" not "the evaluation process"

### Sentence-Level
- Vary length. Three same-length sentences in a row reads as machine output.
- Vary structure. Don't open every sentence with the subject.
- Avoid hedging chains — "It might be the case that perhaps you should consider…"

### Paragraph-Level
- Don't open every paragraph with a transition word
- Don't end every paragraph with a takeaway statement
- Some paragraphs should be one sentence. Some should be six.

### Structural-Level
- Don't follow predictable patterns — "Intro paragraph, three-bullet list, closing paragraph" repeated through a document
- Mix prose and structured elements
- Headers shouldn't all be parallel — "How to X," "How to Y," "How to Z" reads as a content mill

## Zipf's Law Vocabulary Principle

Natural human writing follows Zipf's Law: a small number of high-frequency words make up most of a text. AI tends to over-use mid-frequency academic vocabulary.

Choose the higher-frequency word when both fit:
- "use" not "utilize"
- "show" not "demonstrate"
- "help" not "facilitate"
- "start" not "initiate"
- "end" not "culminate"

## Anti-Pattern Rules (Structural Variety)

Avoid formulaic output. Patterns to break:

1. **The Header Cascade** — H2 → 1-paragraph intro → 3 bullets → 1-paragraph close. Use this once per page maximum.
2. **The Triplet** — every list of three. Two or four is fine.
3. **The Reveal** — "What does this mean? It means…" — answer without setting up.
4. **The Pull-Quote** — sentences engineered to be extractable. Strip them.
5. **The Soft Open** — every section opening with "In today's…" or "When it comes to…"

## Composite Storytelling — Irrelevant Detail Principle

When using a client scenario or archetype example, include one irrelevant specific detail that makes it feel real. "After her third coffee" or "while waiting for the parking validation" or "two days before the holiday weekend" — the detail isn't load-bearing for the point. It's load-bearing for the texture.
