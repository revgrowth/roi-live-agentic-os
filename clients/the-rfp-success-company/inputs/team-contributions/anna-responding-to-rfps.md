## What are the top 5-10 mistakes companies make when responding to RFPs?

1. Not building relationships with potential buyers before an RFP is released.
2. Not reading the full RFP before beginning a response.
   - Not confirming they meet the minimum requirements
   - Not confirming they can meet the experience requirements
   - Not confirming they can provide the services requested
3. Not beginning with a template which includes each question and/or scope of work requirement to be addressed.
   - Not responding to all questions and requirements
4. Using boilerplate content instead of tailoring responses.
5. Not addressing the Buyer and the Buyer's unique situation.
6. Not using the RFP language in the response.
7. Not completing an editorial review, preferably with an outside reviewer.
8. Not defining win themes and differentiators prior to each RFP.
   - Not tailoring win themes and differentiators to the Buyer's needs.
   - Not weaving win themes and differentiators throughout the response.
9. Not being prepared for RFPs by having case studies, client testimonials, and key statistics ready to use.

## What's your philosophy on go/no-go decisions? What criteria matter most?

Using a go/no-go decision tool improves efficiency. It helps you focus time and effort on RFPs that offer a strong chance of success or a useful business development opportunity. By letting go of RFPs that are not a good fit, you can concentrate on the RFPs with the highest potential and increase your win rate. It also lowers the pressure on your team, allowing them to maintain focus on their core responsibilities while responding more effectively to priority bids.

**The criteria that matter most:**

- Is this fully within the scope of what we do?
- Have we done this work in the past?
- Is this a good relationship building opportunity?
- Will the pricing give us our ideal margin?
- Do we have sufficient time and resources to respond?
- Are we going to enjoy working with this client?

**It comes down to a few key points:**

1. Can we do the work requested and prove we can do it?
2. Do we have the time and resources to give our best response?
3. Do we really want to do this work?

## What makes a SLED RFP different from federal or commercial RFPs?

SLED RFPs are driven by local needs and priorities. Knowledge of the region, key players, and existing systems is especially important. There is greater emphasis on the service model and community fit. Providers who already have a local presence or can easily move into a new location have an advantage.

While SLED and Federal RFPs are most often shared publicly, commercial RFPs are usually distributed by invitation only, which means vendors need an established relationship or to be a widely recognized service provider. Evaluation criteria may be most focused on outcomes and ROIs.

Federal RFPs tend to follow stricter formats and emphasize highly structured, fact-based responses, while SLED RFPs can allow more room to make a persuasive case and tailor your response to local priorities. Evaluators are looking for compliance and documented proof of strictly aligned experience.

## What do evaluators actually look for that most bidders miss?

Evaluators want to see that you understand their organization and their specific needs. They look for clear alignment between your solution and their priorities and pain points. They also want confidence that your approach is low risk. That means showing proof you've done similar work before, delivered successful results, and followed a repeatable process that can work for them as well. They want to see past projects and case studies that show a clear path from issue to resolution, not just a list of services provided. They look for specific, practical benefits that your process will deliver for their organization. They also expect supporting evidence such as client testimonials and performance metrics that confirm your capabilities and results.

Strong proposals stay focused on the buyer's needs, not a generic list of vendor features and services. Evaluators also look for direct alignment with the stated evaluation criteria so it is easy to award high scores and justify choosing your company in the final selection.
