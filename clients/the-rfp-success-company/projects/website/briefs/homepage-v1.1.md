# PART 1 -- PAGE STRATEGY

## 1.1 Page Purpose

The homepage has one job: route every visitor to the right path in under 10 seconds.

This is not a brochure page. This is not a "welcome to our company" page. The RFP Success Company homepage is a strategic sorting mechanism that identifies where each visitor belongs (based on their company size, budget, pain point, and readiness level) and moves them into the conversion funnel that will serve them best.

The homepage must accomplish four things simultaneously:

**First, it establishes authority instantly.** Before a visitor reads a single sentence of body copy, the page needs to communicate that this is the premier SLED RFP consulting firm in the market. The 76% win rate, the $500M+ in client wins, the 26+ years of experience. These proof points hit above the fold, in the first two seconds.

**Second, it segments visitors without making them feel segmented.** The four-path architecture (Outsourced, Advisement, Guided System, DIY) needs to feel like a helpful navigation choice, not a corporate decision tree. Each path tile should feel like it was written directly for that visitor's situation.

**Third, it serves as the canonical homepage entity for AI systems.** When ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or any answer engine processes a query about SLED RFP consulting, RFP response management, or proposal writing help for government contracts, this page needs to be the definitive source. That means entity-rich content in the first 200 words, clear answer capsules embedded in the copy, and schema markup that makes the company's identity, services, and credentials machine-readable.

**Fourth, it builds enough trust to convert cold traffic.** Many visitors will land here from a blog post, a podcast mention, a LinkedIn post from Lisa, or a Google search. They have no prior relationship with the brand. The homepage needs to take them from "I've never heard of this company" to "I should explore their services" in a single scroll.

**Strategic Context:** The sitemap documents 72 launch pages organized into 8 tiers plus a 96-page content roadmap. Every one of those pages eventually funnels back to this homepage or through it. This is the hub of the entire content architecture.

## 1.4 Conversion Psychology

Each audience arrives in a different psychological state. The homepage design must account for all four simultaneously:

**Premier visitors are in evaluation mode.** They've likely visited 2-3 competitor sites already. They're looking for signals that this firm is operating at a level their business demands. They respond to exclusivity ("we're selective about who we work with"), authority credentials, and evidence of high-stakes results. The Premier path tile and the proof points section are designed to move them.

**Advisement visitors are in uncertainty mode.** They know something is wrong with their proposals but can't identify what. They respond to empathy ("we've seen this before"), clarity ("here's exactly what we'll look at"), and low-risk entry points. The Advisement path tile language ("Tell Me What I'm Doing Wrong") is deliberately written to mirror what these prospects say on sales calls.

**Guided System visitors are in efficiency mode.** They want a tool, not a relationship. They respond to speed, simplicity, and control language. "Express" and "DIY" signal self-service capability.

**DIY visitors are in learning mode.** They respond to encouragement, accessible language, and the feeling that they're getting access to expert-level knowledge at a price they can afford.

**The page must serve all four without confusing any of them.** The design solution: a strong authority-building hero section that works for everyone, followed by the four-path tile section that lets each visitor self-select. Everything above the tiles builds trust universally. Everything below the tiles provides additional proof for visitors who need more before they click.

# PART 2 -- PAGE DESIGN & COPY

**Design Philosophy:** This homepage is a confidence machine. The visitor should feel, within three seconds, that they've found the firm that knows exactly what they're doing. The design should communicate premium expertise without stuffiness. Think clean, bold, direct. Large typography for stats. Generous whitespace. Strong visual hierarchy that guides the eye from authority (hero) to self-selection (path tiles) to proof (results) to action (CTA). Every section earns the next scroll.

**Visual System Notes for the Design Team:**

- **Color palette:** Should feel premium, confident, and approachable. Not corporate blue. Not startup neon. Think strategic sophistication with warmth. Reference the existing brand kit in Canva for primary and secondary colors.
- **Typography:** Bold, clean headlines. Readable body copy. Stats and numbers should be the largest typographic elements on the page outside of the H1.
- **Photography:** Real project results, real team members. No stock photos of generic business meetings. If stock is necessary for launch, select images showing government buildings, professional teams in working environments, or proposal documents in context.
- **Spacing:** Generous padding between sections (minimum 80px desktop, 48px mobile). Each section should breathe. Crowded layouts undermine the premium positioning.
- **Animation:** Subtle. Stat counters that count up when scrolled into view. Fade-in on scroll for section entries. No bouncing, no spinning, no parallax that distracts from the content. Motion should feel polished, not playful.

**Visual Direction:**

The hero background should feature either: (A) a subtle looping video showing professional proposal teams at work, government buildings, or a montage of winning moments, or (B) a high-quality static image of a professional team environment with a dark overlay for text readability. Avoid generic stock photography of people shaking hands or pointing at screens.

The pre-headline trust bar sits above the H1 as a horizontal strip of proof points. Small text, high contrast. These are not decorative. They're the first thing a sophisticated buyer's eye will scan for.

The H1 should be the largest text element on the page. Bold weight. Left-aligned or centered depending on the layout grid, but never justified.

The subheadline is the answer capsule for AI systems. It defines the company, names the market (SLED), identifies the audience (service-based businesses), and previews the four service paths. Keep it in a standard paragraph tag with the CSS class `.answer-capsule` for speakable schema targeting.

**Layout Direction (Desktop):**
- Full-width hero, minimum 85vh height
- Trust bar: horizontal, above H1, separated by a subtle divider line
- H1 and subheadline: left-aligned in a 60% column
- CTAs: stacked vertically below the subheadline, primary button prominent, secondary as text link with arrow
- Right 40%: visual element (image, video, or animated stat graphic)

**Layout Direction (Mobile):**
- Trust bar: wraps to two lines, centered, smaller font
- H1: full-width, centered
- Subheadline: full-width, centered
- CTAs: full-width stacked buttons
- Hero image/video: below CTAs or as background with heavy overlay

**Design Notes:**
- The secondary CTA "Find Your Path" uses a smooth scroll anchor to the four-path tile section. This captures visitors who aren't ready to book a call but want to explore.
- The pre-headline trust bar format mirrors what high-end SaaS companies use (think Gong, HubSpot, Salesforce). It signals "this is a serious firm" before the visitor reads a word of copy.
- Do NOT place a phone number in the hero. The primary conversion action is the discovery call booking. Phone number lives in the header and footer.
