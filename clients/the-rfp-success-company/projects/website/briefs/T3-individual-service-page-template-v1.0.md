# **PART 1 — TEMPLATE STRATEGY**
## **1.1 Template Purpose**
This template sells a single service. Not a path. Not a methodology overview. Not a company. One service, with enough specificity that a qualified prospect books a discovery call the same day.
Every visitor who lands on a T3 page is deeper in the funnel than visitors on the homepage or the \`/advisement/\` overview. They've already validated: (a) they have a proposal problem, (b) Win Strategist is the right category of solution, and (c) this specific service matches their situation. This template closes the remaining gap — from "this looks like what we need" to "booked the call."
The Service Page SOP calls this the **conversion spine**: hero → trust → problem → solution → proof → process → mid-CTA → testimonials → pricing → FAQ → guarantee → final CTA. T3 implements that spine within the RFP Success® design system.
Three jobs this template must do simultaneously:
**First, it converts.** Per Service Page SOP Phase 1.3, conversion is the primary goal. SEO and AEO signals layer on top without compromising conversion structure. Every section earns its place by contributing to the click on a primary CTA — book a discovery call or take the RFP Assessment quiz.
**Second, it ranks for specific commercial-investigation keywords.** Each of the five services has a different primary keyword. The Evaluator's Eye Audit ranks for "RFP audit services," "proposal audit," "RFP review consultant." The template structure supports ranking without the copywriter thinking about SEO — keyword-first H1, entity-declared schema, AEO-extractable answer capsules in the first 200 words, named methodology anchors.
**Third, it differentiates from competitors selling the same category of work.** The competitive landscape for RFP consulting services is populated by firms positioning as outsourced proposal writers or AI-only tools. Neither fits the Win Strategist model. The "What We Don't Do" framing, evaluator-side credentialing, and 30+ years of Lisa's direct evaluation experience are the differentiation the template must surface — not in a single section but across every section.
**Strategic context:** This template is the first conversion-page template the site will scale (5 uses in launch, possibly more as services expand). Getting it right now sets the pattern for every future service page. The Evaluator's Eye Audit reference build is the pattern. The four other services inherit.

**D.A.R.E. — The Four Audit Dimensions:**

| **Step** | **Dimension** | **What We Look For** |
| ---| ---| --- |
| 1 | \*\*Distinction\*\* | Does the proposal clearly show why this company is the lowest-risk, highest-value choice — not just qualified, but the obvious winner? |

| 4 | \*\*Evidence & Authority\*\* | Does the proposal substantiate every claim with specific, credible, relevant proof — not generic testimonials or resume-style capabilities? |

**Beyond D.A.R.E.: Compliance & Risk Review**
Every audit also checks:
*   **Compliance:** Did the proposal meet every mandatory requirement in the RFP? (A single compliance miss can disqualify an otherwise winning response.)
*   **Risk Signals:** Does the proposal trigger evaluator risk flags — unclear project ownership, weak financial signals, inexperienced-team indicators, insufficient proof of performance?
**The Deliverable:**
After the audit, you receive:
1. In-document comments on every proposal showing specific fixes section by section
2. A strategic report synthesizing patterns across all proposals — the 3–5 highest-impact improvements to apply on your next submission
3. A practical writing guide customized to your industry and contract types
4. A 60-minute live call with your team to walk through findings
