## **TEMPLATE OVERVIEW**
T10 serves two pages with closely related jobs but different emphasis:
**\`/about/\`** — The company's story, mission, values, credentials, and organizational identity. This is the canonical **Organization entity declaration** for the site — parallel to how T9 (\`/lisa-rehurek/\`) is the canonical Person entity declaration.
**\`/team/\`** — Depth of expertise signal without exposing individual bench contractors. Signals organizational capacity and service delivery reliability without turning the page into a generic team grid. Different job than \`/about/\`, solves the "we're more than just Lisa" positioning.
The design system is shared. Content structure differs significantly. Both pages feed Trust and Authoritativeness E-E-A-T signals, but target different prospect questions:
*   **\`/about/\` answers:** "Is this a real, credible company with a clear mission and documented credentials?"
*   **\`/team/\` answers:** "Will the engagement actually get delivered at the quality and depth promised — or is this a solo operator who'll be overwhelmed?"
Three jobs T10 must do across both pages:
**First, it declares the Organization entity for the entire site's knowledge graph.** The \`/about/\` page carries the canonical Organization schema. Every other page on the site references this \`@id\` when mentioning The RFP Success® Company. Get this schema wrong and every page inherits the error — same architectural pattern as T9 for Person.
**Second, it establishes organizational trust.** Per Core Standards Phase 4.4, a transparent About page with real people, photos, location, founding year, and contact information is a required Trust signal on every client site. This isn't optional content — it's table stakes for E-E-A-T, and Google's quality systems evaluate its presence and depth.
**Third, it compresses the proof accumulated across the site into an organizational identity statement.** Every credential badge, every award, every publication, every case study client logo — this page gathers the proof and organizes it around "this is who we are and what we've built."

# **PART 1 — TEMPLATE STRATEGY**
## **1.1 Template Purpose**
\`/about/\` serves a prospect asking "who is this company, and can I trust them with a high-stakes engagement?" It's a mid-funnel trust-verification page, similar in purpose to T9 but with organizational focus rather than expert focus.
Three specific jobs:
**First, \`/about/\` establishes organizational trust through transparency.** Real photos, specific location (Arizona HQ), documented founding year (1998), clear mission, visible values, displayed credentials with external verification. Per Core Standards Phase 4.4, this transparency is required on every client site — and Google's quality systems evaluate its presence.
**Second, \`/about/\` declares the canonical Organization entity.** The schema on this page is the site's Organization declaration per Phase 12.1. Every page (T1 Homepage, T3 service pages, T4 case studies, T5 industry pages, T9 author bio) references THIS page's Organization \`@id\`.
**Third, \`/about/\` compresses the brand's narrative into organizational identity.** Who we serve (SLED service-based businesses), what we do (Win Strategist advisory), how we're different (evaluator-side expertise, 4x4 Framework™), what we believe (RIDE OR DIE values), what we've earned (credentials, awards, track record). A prospect who reads \`/about/\` should be able to summarize The RFP Success® Company to a colleague in three sentences.
\`/team/\` has a different job. A prospect on \`/team/\` is asking: "Can this firm deliver at the scale and depth they're claiming? Or is Lisa doing everything herself, which means my engagement will compete for her attention?" The answer is neither "here's our 30-person team with headshots" (inaccurate — bench contractors aren't public-facing) nor "it's just Lisa" (undersells the delivery model). The page has to signal organizational capacity without exposing individual contractors.
**Strategic context:** T9 and T10 together form the site's E-E-A-T anchor pair. T9 = Person entity (Lisa's expertise and experience). T10 = Organization entity (the company's trust and authoritativeness). Every service page, case study, industry page, and blog post on the site references both entities via schema. Getting both right now pays compounding returns across the entire 165-page site.

## **1.4 Target Audience**
Three reader types arrive at T10 pages:
### **Audience A: The Verifier (same as T9 Audience A)**
Came from a service page or case study. Mid-funnel. Trust-verifying. Scans for:
*   Real company (Arizona HQ, 1998 founding year)
*   Real credentials (Inc. 5000 with verification)
*   Real mission (SLED specialist, not generalist)
*   Real values (RIDE OR DIE)
Once verified, returns to the service page or books a discovery call.
### **Audience B: The Delivery-Risk Evaluator**
Primarily arrives on \`/team/\`. Asking: "If I sign a $18K engagement, will it get delivered at the quality promised? Is Lisa doing everything herself?"
Wants to see: organizational capacity signals, quality control processes, senior involvement guarantees, and honest framing about the delivery model.
### **Audience C: The Journalist / Partner / Press**
Same as T9 Audience C, but organization-focused rather than expert-focused. Looking for company history, organizational credentials, press contact, partnership inquiry path.
## **1.5 Conversion Psychology**
\`/about/\` converts through coherence. Every signal on the page must reinforce one central identity: "The RFP Success® Company is the premier SLED Win Strategist advisory firm, founded by Lisa Rehurek, with documented track record, named values, and specific service scope."
If the values conflict with the tone, trust breaks. If the credentials look padded, trust breaks. If the mission contradicts the services pages, trust breaks.
Three principles:
1. **Coherence over comprehensiveness.** A tight, consistent identity statement converts better than an exhaustive list of everything the company does. Cut content that doesn't reinforce the central identity.
2. **Specific credentials over generic superlatives.** "Inc. 5000 (2023, 2024)" with an external verification link beats "award-winning." "WBENC-certified Woman Business Owner" beats "diversity-focused." Every credential carries a verification path.
3. **Values that sound like someone actually said them.** RIDE OR DIE sounds like Lisa. "Adore Honesty" and "Get Sh!t Done" read as authentic because they're specific. Generic corporate values ("Integrity, Excellence, Innovation") would break the trust the rest of the page builds.

**Section 4 — Why This Model:**
H2: "Why We Don't Run a Large W-2 Team"
Body copy:
The RFP Success® Company has grown to 30+ industries and $500M+ in client wins without building a large W-2 employee team. That's intentional.
Building a full-time staff to absorb client demand creates two problems:
*   **Capacity outpaces demand.** A full-time team requires constant client flow to justify salaries. The pressure to fill engagement slots can compromise fit — taking on clients who aren't a match, for engagements that aren't right for them.
*   **Quality compromises during transitions.** Staff turnover produces quality inconsistency. Contractors who've worked together on multiple engagements (and have deep proposal expertise outside of The RFP Success® Company) produce more consistent work than a staff rebuilding after each departure.
The bench contractor model means we scale capacity when client demand justifies it — without pressure to take on engagements that don't fit. That's why we've maintained a 76%+ win rate as the client portfolio has expanded.
