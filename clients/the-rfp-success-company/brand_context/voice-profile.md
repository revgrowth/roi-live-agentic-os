## Lisa Rehurek — founder voice

- Builds from pain point → insight → actionable solution

### Stop Slop overlay (inherited from agency Core Standards §8)

These apply to all RFP Success copy in addition to the brand-specific rules above:

- No "Here's the thing," "Let that sink in," "The uncomfortable truth is," "It's worth noting," "Let me be clear," "At the end of the day," "And that's okay"
- No "It's not this, but that," "Lean into," "Unpack," "Double down," "Deep dive," "Game-changer," "Move the needle"
- No "Navigate the complex landscape," "Data-driven" (as filler), "At its core," "In today's world"
- No "When it comes to," "It turns out that," "The reality is," "What makes X unique," "This is where it gets interesting"
- No binary contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y."), dramatic one-word fragments ("Purpose. Clarity. Impact."), negative listings ("Not because X. Because Y.")
- No em-dash reveals, rhetorical questions answered immediately, pull-quote-ready sentences
- Three-item rhetorical lists banned (use two); factual enumerations of three permitted per Core Standards §8.2 (April 29, 2026 clarification)
- Kill all -ly adverbs and softeners (just, simply, actually, really, very, quite, perhaps, certainly)
- Kill passive voice and narrator-from-a-distance voice ("One might argue")

**Conflict resolution:** Stop Slop bans em-dashes when used for reveals; Lisa's natural speech includes them. In Lisa's first-person ghostwritten posts (LinkedIn, blog under her byline), em-dashes are permitted where they reflect her cadence. In all other channels, follow Stop Slop.

### AI signal elimination framework

In addition to banned phrases, every paragraph must pass these four-level checks (from RFP Success Website & Content SOP v1.2):

**Word-level**

- No "delve," "navigate," "unlock," "harness," "leverage," "robust," "seamless," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class"
- No empty modifiers — "truly," "really," "very," "incredibly"
- Use specific nouns over abstract ones — "the evaluator's scoring sheet" not "the evaluation process"

**Sentence-level**

- Vary length. Three same-length sentences in a row reads as machine output.
- Vary structure. Don't open every sentence with the subject.
- Avoid hedging chains — "It might be the case that perhaps you should consider…"

**Paragraph-level**

- Don't open every paragraph with a transition word
- Don't end every paragraph with a takeaway statement
- Some paragraphs should be one sentence. Some should be six.

**Structural-level**

- Don't follow predictable patterns — "Intro paragraph, three-bullet list, closing paragraph" repeated
- Mix prose and structured elements
- Headers shouldn't all be parallel — "How to X," "How to Y," "How to Z" reads as a content mill

### Zipf's Law vocabulary principle

Natural human writing follows Zipf's Law: a small number of high-frequency words make up most of a text. AI tends to over-use mid-frequency academic vocabulary. Choose the higher-frequency word when both fit:

- "use" not "utilize"
- "show" not "demonstrate"
- "help" not "facilitate"
- "start" not "initiate"
- "end" not "culminate"

### Anti-pattern rules (structural variety)

Avoid formulaic output. Patterns to break:

1. **The Header Cascade** — H2 → 1-paragraph intro → 3 bullets → 1-paragraph close. Use this once per page maximum.
2. **The Triplet** — every list of three. Two or four is fine.
3. **The Reveal** — "What does this mean? It means…" — answer without setting up.
4. **The Pull-Quote** — sentences engineered to be extractable. Strip them.
5. **The Soft Open** — every section opening with "In today's…" or "When it comes to…"

### Composite storytelling — irrelevant detail principle

When using a client scenario or archetype example, include one irrelevant specific detail that makes it feel real. "After her third coffee" or "while waiting for the parking validation" or "two days before the holiday weekend" — the detail isn't load-bearing for the point. It's load-bearing for the texture.

## Content structure patterns

**Lisa article formula:**

1. Hook with pain point or question
2. Share a brief story or example
3. Deliver an insight or framework
4. Provide actionable takeaways
5. End with encouragement and a CTA

**LinkedIn post structure:** Hook → Insight → Story/Example → Takeaway → CTA.

**Email structure:** Provocative subject → direct opening → one key insight or offer → clear CTA.

**Value proposition structure:** Target audience → problem statement → solution → benefits → differentiation.

## Readability targets (from D.A.R.E. methodology)

- Target reading level: **6th grade**
- Sentence length: 8-word sentences = 100% comprehension. 43-word sentences = <10% comprehension.
- Use subheaders, bullets, and the client's own language.
- Client-Centric Sentence Formula: *(Client) can improve/achieve/reduce (challenge) by using our (solution) which has already been proven on (relevant experience/program) and resulted in (metric).*
