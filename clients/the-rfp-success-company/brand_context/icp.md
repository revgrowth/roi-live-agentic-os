# Ideal Customer Profile — The RFP Success® Company

**Source basis:** Master Brand Voice & DNA Guide v2.2, Sitemap v9, four Ideal Client Clarity Worksheets (Library Content, Strategic Review, Content Refinement, Play to Win).

The RFP Success® Company operates a tiered ICP model. Tier governs which path the prospect enters on the homepage, which services they see, and which voice register the content uses.

## The five customer archetypes

Every page must speak to at least one of these archetypes. Archetypes cut across the revenue tiers — a Champagne client and a Wine client can both be Almost-Winners. Use the archetype to drive copy emotional resonance; use the segment to drive offer routing.

### 1. The Scrambler

Reactive, deadline-driven, no defined process. Every RFP is a fire drill. Pulls in subject-matter experts at the last minute. Burned out by response fatigue.

**Pain language:** "We scramble every time." "We don't have a process, we just respond." "We waste time chasing everything."

**Best-fit service:** Play to Win Consults (process foundation), Library Content Development (reduces scramble per RFP).

### 2. The Almost-Winner

Strong shortlist rate — 70%+ down-selection — but win conversion stays low. Has the qualifications. Has the experience. Doesn't know why proposals don't close.

**Pain language:** "We keep losing but don't know why." "We're competitive but not winning." "We get to the finals and then it goes the other way."

**Best-fit service:** Evaluator's Eye Audit (entry), Win Strategy Review (deeper diagnostic).

### 3. The Capability Dumper

Writes about the company instead of the buyer. Heavy on capabilities, light on outcomes. Generic positioning. Sounds like everyone else. "It's a data dump, not a story."

**Pain language:** "Our SMEs don't know how to write strategically." "We sound like everyone else." "It sounds like a data dump."

**Best-fit service:** Embedded Content Refinement (the flagship), Win Strategy Review.

### 4. The First-Timer

Pursuing their first SLED contract. Doesn't know what they don't know. Underestimates the time required. May not yet understand procurement-side culture.

**Pain language:** "We've never done this before." "We don't know where to start." "Is this even a winnable opportunity for us?"

**Best-fit service:** Play to Win Consults (foundation), Library Content Development (baseline content), Evaluator's Eye Audit (after first draft).

### 5. The Internal Champion

Has executive buy-in but needs ammunition to defend the spend. Often a Proposal Manager or BD Lead who knows they need help and is building the internal case.

**Pain language:** "I need to show leadership why this matters." "I need to justify the investment." "I need data, not vibes."

**Best-fit service:** Evaluator's Eye Audit (clear deliverable to show), Play to Win Consults (strategic roadmap to defend), case studies and ROI calculator (justification ammunition).

## Win Strategist — Wine 🍷

**Tagline:** *Smart, scrappy teams who want a Win Strategist without overcomplicating things.*

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $5M–$10M (Locked April 2026 via C15) |
| Contract size | $100K–$250K |
| Team posture | Smaller teams, sometimes accidental proposal owners |
| Budget | Tighter, ROI-focused, disciplined |
| Mindset | Practical, efficiency-driven |
| Engagement preference | DIY or advisory; one-time expert insight |
| What they need | Frameworks, tools, templates, structure that saves time and reduces chaos |
| RFP philosophy | Improve outcomes practically, not transform the business model |

**Wine sees:** Evaluator's Eye Audit, Library Content Development, Win Strategy Review (entry level), Play to Win Consults, plus full product catalog (courses, playbooks, books).

**Wine does NOT see:** Embedded Content Refinement (Champagne-only premium service).

**Wine voice register:** Practical. Approachable. Action-oriented.

**Wine service progression:**

- **Entry:** Evaluator's Eye Audit (Lite) → Full Evaluator's Eye Audit
- **Growth:** Library Content → Win Strategy Review
- **Strategic:** Play to Win

---

## Win Strategist — Champagne 🥂

**Tagline:** *Serious operators who invest in a Win Strategist and value the partnership.*

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $10M–$25M+ (Locked April 2026 via C15) |
| Contract size | $250K+ |
| Team posture | Established proposal team, growth-minded |
| Budget | Willing to invest at premium for deep expertise |
| Mindset | RFPs are a strategic revenue engine, not a last-minute task |
| Engagement preference | High-touch strategic partnership, senior-level access |
| What they need | Strategic insight beyond the document, advisement that challenges assumptions, systems that compound results |
| RFP philosophy | Bid selectively; treat each pursuit as a strategic investment |

**Champagne sees:** Full Evaluator's Eye Audit, Embedded Content Refinement ($12K–$18K), Win Strategy Review ($7.5K–$10K), Library Content, Play to Win Consults.

**Champagne does NOT see:** Courses, playbooks, books, most downloadable products. Premium tier is products-free.

**Champagne voice register:** Formal. Consultative. Strategic. "Mercedes." Investment language.

**Champagne service progression:**

- **Entry:** Full Evaluator's Eye Audit ($2,500–$5,000)
- **Growth:** Library Content ($4,200) → Embedded Content Refinement ($12K–$18K)
- **Strategic:** Play to Win ($7,500–$12,000)

## Wine vs. Champagne — quiz segmentation criteria

The Win Strategist Quiz uses six criteria to sort:

1. Company revenue → $5M–$10M (Wine) vs. $10M–$25M+ (Champagne)
2. Contract size → $100K–$250K (Wine) vs. $250K+ (Champagne)
3. Team size and internal capacity → smaller/scrappier (Wine) vs. established (Champagne)
4. Engagement preference → DIY/advisory/one-time (Wine) vs. high-touch partnership (Champagne)
5. Budget posture → tighter/disciplined (Wine) vs. premium investment (Champagne)
6. RFP philosophy → improve outcomes practically (Wine) vs. RFPs as strategic revenue engine (Champagne)

## Offer-specific buyer profiles

The four Ideal Client Clarity Worksheets give offer-level detail. Use these for individual service page targeting.

### Evaluator's Eye Audit (formerly Proposal Audit) — $2,500–$5,000

**Common entry point for Advisement clients.**

**Who:** CEO, COO, Proposal Manager, BD Lead — teams that keep losing without knowing why.

**Their pain language:**

- "We keep losing but don't know why."
- "We need expert eyes."
- "We want honest feedback, not fluff."
- "We don't know what we don't know."

**Triggers:** A series of losses. Leadership pressure. A high-growth target requiring smarter pursuits.

---

### Win Strategy Review (formerly Strategic Review) — $7,500–$10,000

**Champagne only.**

**Who:** Teams that can write well but need expert-level strategic feedback early enough to course-correct. Bidding on a highly competitive RFP, or coming off a big loss.

**Buyer roles:** CEO, COO, Proposal Manager, BD Lead.

**Their pain language:**

- "We don't know if this is competitive."
- "We sound like everyone else."
- "Not sure we're hitting what evaluators care about."
- "Writing is too technical."
- "We don't know how to tell our story so it connects with evaluators."

**Triggers:** Must-win RFP. Leadership pressure. Awareness that "good enough" isn't.

**Green-flag traits:** Values strategy and partnership. Submits drafts on time. Collaborates. Wants early guidance. Seeks clarity, not just corrections.

**Red flags:** Unresponsive SMEs. Expects a full rewrite. Chronically late with drafts. Wants to do it their way.

---

### Embedded Content Refinement (formerly Content Refinement) — $12,000–$18,000

**Champagne only. Premium Win Strategist offering.**

**Who:** Teams that can draft technical content but need expert-level refinement to make it evaluator-ready. They can write technically but not persuasively.

**Buyer roles:** CEO, COO, VP Sales/BD, Proposal Manager.

**Their pain language:**

- "Our SMEs don't know how to write strategically."
- "We're not standing out."
- "It sounds like a data dump, not a story."
- "May meet the basic requirements, but it doesn't persuade."
- "We have the experience, but our proposals don't show it."

**Triggers:** A last-straw loss. Must-win opportunity. Overwhelmed team. Leadership insisting the proposal "needs to sound better."

**Green-flag traits:** Complete technical drafts. Responsive SMEs. Open to edits. Upcoming opportunity. Desire to improve.

**Red flags:** Draft not ready. Limited SME availability. Expects full rewrite. Unclear scope. "Just make it sound better" without their input.

---

### Play to Win Consults — $7,500–$12,000

**Wine and Champagne.**

**Who:** Teams that want clarity, direction, and a strategic blueprint for winning consistently. Often reactive, overwhelmed, and disorganized.

**Buyer roles:** CEO, COO, VP Sales/BD, GovCon Lead, Proposal Manager — anyone responsible for revenue strategy.

**Their pain language:**

- "We don't know who to chase."
- "We're guessing."
- "We waste time on opportunities we shouldn't pursue."
- "We don't have a win strategy."
- "We don't have a process, we just respond."
- "We're chasing everything and winning almost nothing."
- "We want to win smarter, not work harder."

**Triggers:** A series of losses. Leadership pressure. A high-growth goal requiring smarter pursuits.

**Green-flag traits:** Open to feedback. Ready for structure. Has some RFP history. Bids on multiple RFPs/year. Team engaged on calls.

**Red flags:** Leadership resistant to narrowing focus. Defensive about current processes or content. Unwilling to adopt selection criteria. Expecting done-for-you instead of partnership.

---

### Library Content Development — $4,200 (6 sections)

**Wine and Champagne. Often the first product after an Audit.**

**Who:** Teams without a content library, businesses writing RFPs ad hoc, proposal teams needing strong baseline content, companies heavily reliant on SMEs to write/rewrite content, companies increasing RFP volume.

**Buyer roles:** CEO, COO, Proposal Manager, BD Lead — whoever owns proposal creation.

**Their pain language:**

- "We rewrite the same things over and over."
- "Our boilerplate is outdated."
- "Our content is no longer competitive."
- "Our library is a mess."
- "Our SMEs are overloaded with content requests."

**Triggers:** A big upcoming RFP. Internal frustration. Leadership demanding better proposal quality.

**Green-flag traits:** Has SME knowledge. Wants reusable content. Open to new writing styles and best practices. Sees RFPs as revenue drivers.

**Red flags:** Can't make decisions. Can't provide basic information. No alignment on messaging. Expects content with no SME input. Defensive about their existing content.

## Cross-segment common ground

Across every Win Strategist tier, the ideal client:

- Sees RFPs as a revenue driver, not an interruption
- Has SME knowledge worth extracting
- Will provide drafts or interviews on time
- Values evaluator-perspective expertise
- Wants honest, candid feedback over polite agreement

Across every Win Strategist tier, the disqualifier is:

- Unresponsive SMEs and missed deadlines
- Wants done-for-you in the discontinued FRM sense ("just make us something")
- Treating the engagement as transactional
- Defensive about existing process or messaging
