## 1. THE HIERARCHY OF PRIORITIES

When rules conflict, this is the order:

1. **Sound like Lisa Rehurek.** Brand voice wins over every structural requirement.
2. **Add information gain.** Every page contains knowledge competing pages don't have.
3. **Tell stories with real humans.** Named clients, evaluator perspectives, specific proposal outcomes.
4. **Get entity SEO right.** Brand name density, Lisa attribution, schema, Win Strategist positioning.
5. **Get technical SEO right.** Keyword placement, linking, metadata, AEO/GEO capsules.

## 2. INFORMATION GAIN — APPLIED TO RFP SUCCESS

### The Delta Test
Before any page publishes: "If this page disappeared from the internet, would anyone lose access to something they couldn't find elsewhere?" If no, the page needs more substance.

### The Deferral Rule
If a page can't reach HIGH information gain, defer publication until it can.

### HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW Assessment

**HIGH (publish):** Page has 5+ client-specific information gain elements
**MEDIUM (defer):** Page has 3 elements with at least 1 client-specific
**LOW (rework):** Page is AI-synthesizable from public web content

### RFP Success-Specific Sources of Information Gain

1. **Evaluator-side expertise** — Lisa, Susan, Anna perspectives from inside the evaluation room
2. **4x4 Framework™ and D.A.R.E. Method** — proprietary methodology applied to specific client situations
3. **Named client outcomes** — Optumas 86%, Highstreet IT $8.5M, RTA Fleet 50%→80%, Modern Learners $500K→$1M
4. **Real sales call patterns** — actual prospect questions, objections, decision factors from Lisa's transcripts
5. **Industry-specific evaluator behavior** — across 30+ verticals
6. **State-specific procurement rules and rhythms** — Texas, California, Florida, NY, PA, OH, GA, IL
7. **Composite storytelling** — with the irrelevant detail principle

## 3. HUMAN VOICE, STORYTELLING & FIVE CUSTOMER ARCHETYPES

### Pronoun Rule
Zero bare "we" in first 200 words. "The RFP Success® Company" or "RFP-expert Lisa Rehurek" replaces "we" until entity is established. Lisa's voice — she names things directly.

### The Five Customer Archetypes

Every page speaks to at least one:

1. **The Scrambler** — Reactive, deadline-driven, no defined process
   - Voice: empathetic, structure-promising
   - Hooks: "the proposal due Friday," "after 11pm submissions"

2. **The Almost-Winner** — Strong shortlist rate (70%+), low conversion, doesn't know why
   - Voice: diagnostic, "we see why"
   - Hooks: "downselected three times," "qualified but didn't win"

3. **The Capability Dumper** — Writes about themselves instead of the buyer
   - Voice: corrective, evaluator-side
   - Hooks: "look at us, look at us," "vomit information about your company"

4. **The First-Timer** — Pursuing first SLED contract
   - Voice: instructive, hand-on-shoulder
   - Hooks: "your first government RFP," "the differences between SLED and commercial"

5. **The Internal Champion** — Has buy-in but needs ammunition to defend the spend
   - Voice: ROI-framed, justification-supplying
   - Hooks: "$250K opportunity cost," "competitor pricing $35K–$55K"

### Composite Storytelling — Irrelevant Detail Principle

When using a client scenario, include one irrelevant specific detail that makes it feel real. "After her third coffee" or "two days before the holiday weekend" — the detail isn't load-bearing for the point. It's load-bearing for the texture.

## 6. ENTITY SEO RULES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

### Brand Name in First Sentence
Every page's opening sentence contains "The RFP Success® Company" + the primary keyword. Not "first paragraph" — first sentence.

### Founder Attribution Rule
First mention on any page: **"RFP-expert Lisa Rehurek"** (per Master Feedback Tracker §2.2). Subsequent mentions drop the prefix. First mention links to `/lisa-rehurek/`.

### Primary Entity Visibility Rule
The page's topic must be identifiable from the H1 alone. JSON-LD `about` must match the H1.

### Trademark Rule
**The RFP Success® Company** — ® symbol required on every public-facing brand mention. Not stylistic — legal requirement per Lisa April 2026.

### Methodology Trademarks
- 4x4 Framework™
- D.A.R.E. Method (no symbol, but always capitalized exactly as `D.A.R.E.`)

### Entity Density (Natural Usage)
- No minimum instance counts
- Use the forced-insertion test
- Default: brand name appears every 150–200 words in conversational sections
- Lisa Rehurek appears at least once per page, linked to bio on first mention

## 7. AEO / GEO OPTIMIZATION RULES

### Answer Capsules Per Page
- 5–10 capsules with `.answer-capsule` CSS class
- Each capsule 1–3 sentences
- Each capsule self-contained (no "as mentioned above")
- Capsules placed in flow, not stacked

### Direct Answer Rule
Every H2 opens with a 1–3 sentence direct answer.

### Bullet List Rule
The D.A.R.E. dimensions render as bullets. The 4x4 pillars render as bullets. The 5 archetypes render as bullets. AI extractors prefer them.

### Speakable Schema Targets
- `.hero-quote` — for testimonial pulls
- `.answer-capsule` — for direct-answer passages

## 8. CONTENT WRITING RULES & BRAND VOICE COMPLIANCE

### Words TO USE
Win, succeed, capture, secure, award, strategic, intentional, deliberate, positioned, aligned, partner, collaborate, expert, seasoned, proven, results, outcomes, impact, ROI, confident, empowered

### Words TO AVOID (Brand-Specific)
Fail, lose, risk (as scare), guarantee, promise, ensure (re: winning), maybe, possibly, might, synergize, leverage, paradigm, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, affordable, cheap, discount

### Lisa-Specific Phrases (Use These)
- "Your team writes it. We make it win."
- "You can't see the picture when you're inside the frame."
- "AI writes words. We write wins."
- "IT IS NOT ABOUT YOU."
- "SLED-specialist rigor, built for state and local speed."
- "Do Better. Win More.®"
- "Cheers to your success" (Lisa's standard sign-off)

### Tone by Audience
- **Wine (Wine Strategist — smaller)**: Practical, approachable, action-oriented, "smart and scrappy"
- **Champagne (Win Strategist — larger)**: Consultative, strategic, "Mercedes"
- **Express (Guided System)**: Direct, value-forward, accessible
- **DIY**: Educational, encouraging, "you've got this"

## 9. AI SIGNAL ELIMINATION — CLIENT-SPECIFIC

### Banned Words (Beyond Agency Baseline)
- "Outsourced" (in positive context — discontinued service)
- "Federal" (in positive context — not a market)
- "Proposal writing help" (use "Win Strategist services")
- "Cheap" / "Affordable" / "Discount" (premium positioning)

### Banned Concepts
- Fear-based marketing ("you'll fail without us")
- Guaranteed wins
- Self-aggrandizement ("look at us, look at us")
- Generic capability claims without proof

### Word-Level Variety
Strip "delve," "navigate," "unlock," "harness," "leverage," "robust," "seamless"

### Sentence-Level Variety
- Three same-length sentences in a row → break one
- Don't open every sentence with the subject
- Don't end every paragraph with a takeaway

### Paragraph-Level Variety
- Some paragraphs: one sentence
- Some paragraphs: six sentences
- Mix prose and structured elements

### Structural-Level Variety
- Don't follow "intro paragraph → three bullets → close" template through a whole document
- Headers shouldn't all be parallel — "How to X, How to Y, How to Z" reads as content mill

## 10. ANTI-PATTERN RULES

Patterns to break:

1. **The Header Cascade** — once per page max
2. **The Triplet** — use two or four
3. **The Reveal** — answer without setup
4. **The Pull-Quote** — strip extractable-engineered sentences
5. **The Soft Open** — vary section openings

### The Contrarian Rule
Every page should contain one statement that contradicts conventional industry wisdom. "Most RFP firms tell you to bid more. We tell you to bid less."

## 16. Q1 2026 Provenance — Verbatim Phrasings from Original Project Knowledge

Four verbatim Q1 2026 phrasings from the original Claude.ai project knowledge dump are preserved here for provenance. Each was identified during the May 12, 2026 Step 4 agency-SOP reconciliation as RFP-Success-specific content that should not be promoted to the agency-level SOPs. The underlying rules are already covered elsewhere in this document — these are the original phrasings, attached to the broader rules that govern them, so future sessions can apply the canonical Q1 wording when it fits the work. This section is reference-only. It does not introduce new rules. When a Q1 phrasing fits the current work, use it; when it doesn't, the canonical rules in §1–§15 take precedence.

### 16.1 — Layer 1 voice example: "Body copy that reads as Lisa/the founder would speak it"

**Verbatim from dump:**

> **Layer 1 (visible):** Body copy that reads as Lisa/the founder would speak it
> **Layer 2 (extractable):** Answer capsules, FAQPage entries, stat blocks with explicit CSS targeting

**Dump source:** `AGENCY_CORE_STANDARDS_v1.1.md` (Q1 snapshot) — `PHASE 6: AEO / GEO / AIEO OPTIMIZATION PATTERNS` → `Two-Layer Content Architecture`.

**Application for RFP Success:** This Q1 phrasing names Lisa as the voice anchor for Layer 1 (the visible body). It maps directly to §1.1 of this SOP ("Sound like Lisa Rehurek") and the full §3 Human Voice treatment. When building any RFP Success page, treat the two-layer pattern as: Layer 1 = body copy in Lisa's voice per §3 and §8; Layer 2 = the AEO/GEO extractable layer specified in §7 (answer capsules with `.answer-capsule`, hero quotes with `.hero-quote`, outcome stats with `.outcome-stats`).

### 16.2 — Service Definition Lock parent-path phrasing

**Verbatim from dump:**

> ### 1.1 — Service Definition Lock
> Before any copy, confirm:
> - Service name (current, no legacy aliases)
> - Pricing model (range, "starting at," or custom quote)
> - Deliverables list (what client gets, what client does, what client does NOT get)
> - Ideal client revenue range
> - Typical engagement timeline
> - Alignment with parent path (Win Strategist / Express / DIY-equivalent)

**Dump source:** `AGENCY_SERVICE_PAGE_SOP_v1.1.md` (Q1 snapshot) — `PHASE 1: PRE-BUILD RESEARCH` → `§1.1 Service Definition Lock`.

**Application for RFP Success:** "Win Strategist / Express / DIY" is the RFP Success tier hierarchy per Master Feedback Tracker §1.10 (Express $1M–$5M), §1.11 (DIY $500K–$1M), and §3.2 (Win Strategist as proprietary positioning term). Before drafting any service page, lock which parent path the service belongs to, then map the audience and voice to Wine / Champagne / Express / DIY per §8 Tone by Audience of this SOP. The "Service Definition Lock" checklist itself is a useful pre-build research artifact even when working outside service pages.

### 16.3 — Problem framing example: the Almost-Winner

**Verbatim from dump:**

> ### 3.2 — Problem Framing
> - Speak to one specific archetype's pain. Not "businesses that struggle with X" — "the Almost-Winner with a 70%+ shortlist rate but a 25% close rate."
> - One specific scenario. Composite storytelling with irrelevant detail.
> - End with the bridge to solution. No fear-based language.

**Dump source:** `AGENCY_SERVICE_PAGE_SOP_v1.1.md` (Q1 snapshot) — `PHASE 3: SECTION COMPOSITION RULES` → `§3.2 Problem Framing`.

**Application for RFP Success:** The Almost-Winner is archetype #2 of five defined in §3 of this SOP ("Strong shortlist rate (70%+), low conversion, doesn't know why"). The dump's exact phrasing ("70%+ shortlist rate but a 25% close rate") puts numbers on the archetype and is reusable directly in problem-framing copy. Apply on every service page and case-study problem-framing block: name the specific archetype with concrete numbers; never use generic "businesses that struggle with X" framing. The companion rule (composite storytelling with irrelevant detail) is covered in §3 of this SOP.

### 16.4 — Pricing Treatment C: Champagne-tier custom-quote wording

**Verbatim from dump:**

> ### 3.6 — Pricing
> Three valid treatments:
> - **Treatment A:** Range displayed (`$X,XXX–$XX,XXX`). Best for fixed-scope services.
> - **Treatment B:** "Starting at $X,XXX" with rationale. Best for scope-variable services.
> - **Treatment C:** "Custom quote based on RFP complexity." Best for enterprise/Champagne tier.

**Dump source:** `AGENCY_SERVICE_PAGE_SOP_v1.1.md` (Q1 snapshot) — `PHASE 3: SECTION COMPOSITION RULES` → `§3.6 Pricing`.

**Application for RFP Success:** Pricing Treatment C wording is reserved for Win Strategist Champagne-tier engagements (the consultative "Mercedes" tier per §8 Tone by Audience of this SOP) where scope varies with RFP complexity rather than fitting a fixed-scope or starting-at frame. Use the verbatim "Custom quote based on RFP complexity" on Champagne-tier service page pricing blocks. Wine tier (smaller Win Strategist), Express tier, and DIY tier use Treatment A or B per their voice profiles and scope conventions. Document the chosen treatment per service in the Client Parameter Sheet, consistent with the agency-baseline Service Page SOP §10.
